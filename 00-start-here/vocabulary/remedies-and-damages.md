# Remedies And Damages

Back to the [vocabulary overview](./README.md).

What a winning party actually gets: money, injunctions, restitution, equitable relief, and limits on recovery.

### `adequate remedy at law`

**Remedies / Equity**

A legal remedy, usually money damages, that fully and practically fixes the injury. If damages are adequate, equity usually will not grant extraordinary relief like an injunction or specific performance.

*In use:* The court denied specific performance because the buyer had an adequate remedy at law.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/adequate_remedy) · related: equitable remedy, injunction, specific performance

### `additur`

**Remedies / Civil Procedure**

A trial judge's increase of a jury's damages award, usually offered as an alternative to a new trial. Federal courts do not use additur because of Seventh Amendment limits, though some state courts allow it.

*In use:* The state court ordered additur after finding the verdict unreasonably low.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/additur) · related: remittitur, damages, jury trial

### `compensatory damages`

**Remedies**

Money awarded to make the plaintiff whole for actual loss. They can cover economic loss, physical injury, pain and suffering, and other legally recognized harms.

*In use:* The jury awarded compensatory damages for medical bills and lost wages.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/compensatory_damages) · related: damages, nominal damages, punitive damages

### `consequential damages`

**Remedies / Contracts**

Damages for losses that flow from the breach because of the plaintiff's particular circumstances, beyond the immediate value of the broken promise. They are usually limited by foreseeability.

*In use:* The buyer sought consequential damages for lost resale profits.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/consequential_damages) · related: expectation damages, foreseeability, mitigation

### `constructive trust`

**Remedies / Equity**

An equitable remedy treating a person who wrongfully holds property as a trustee for the rightful owner. It prevents unjust enrichment when property was obtained or kept unfairly.

*In use:* The court imposed a constructive trust over the diverted settlement funds.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/constructive_trust) · related: unjust enrichment, restitution, equitable remedy

### `declaratory judgment`

**Remedies / Civil Procedure**

A judgment declaring the parties' legal rights without necessarily ordering damages or coercive relief. It is useful when parties need a legal answer before acting.

*In use:* The insurer sought a declaratory judgment that it had no duty to defend.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/declaratory_judgment) · related: injunction, justiciability, remedy

### `disgorgement`

**Remedies / Restitution**

A remedy requiring a wrongdoer to give up profits gained through misconduct. It focuses on the defendant's gain rather than the plaintiff's loss.

*In use:* The court ordered disgorgement of profits from the insider trading scheme.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/disgorgement) · related: restitution, unjust enrichment, accounting

### `equitable lien`

**Remedies / Equity**

An equitable charge against specific property to secure payment of a debt or obligation. It does not transfer title, but it gives the claimant a remedy tied to the property.

*In use:* The contractor sought an equitable lien on the improved parcel.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/equitable_remedy) · related: constructive trust, restitution, lien

### `equitable remedy`

**Remedies / Equity**

A non-monetary remedy rooted in equity, such as injunction, specific performance, rescission, or reformation. Courts usually reserve equitable remedies for situations where legal remedies are inadequate.

*In use:* Because damages could not protect the trade secret, the plaintiff sought an equitable remedy.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/equitable_remedy) · related: adequate remedy at law, injunction, specific performance

### `expectation damages`

**Remedies / Contracts**

Contract damages that put the injured party in the position they would have occupied if the contract had been performed. This is the standard measure for breach of contract.

*In use:* Expectation damages gave the seller the profit it expected from the deal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/expectation_damages) · related: reliance damages, consequential damages, mitigation

### `injunction`

**Remedies / Equity**

A court order requiring a party to do something or stop doing something. Injunctions are equitable remedies, commonly tied to irreparable harm and inadequate legal remedies.

*In use:* The court issued an injunction barring further use of the trademark.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/injunction) · related: preliminary injunction, permanent injunction, contempt

### `irreparable harm`

**Remedies / Equity**

Harm that cannot be fully fixed by money damages after the fact. Showing irreparable harm is central to many requests for preliminary injunctive relief.

*In use:* Disclosure of the confidential files would cause irreparable harm.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/irreparable_harm) · related: injunction, adequate remedy at law, preliminary injunction

### `laches`

**Remedies / Equity**

An equitable defense based on unreasonable delay that prejudices the defendant. It resembles a statute of limitations but is flexible and fairness-based.

*In use:* Laches barred the injunction after the owner waited years to sue.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/laches) · related: equity, statute of limitations, estoppel

### `liquidated damages`

**Remedies / Contracts**

A contract term setting damages in advance if breach occurs. Courts enforce it when it reasonably estimates hard-to-measure loss, but not when it operates as a penalty.

*In use:* The lease contained a liquidated damages clause for early termination.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/liquidated_damages) · related: penalty, expectation damages, contract

### `mitigation of damages`

**Remedies**

The injured party's duty to take reasonable steps to reduce avoidable loss. A plaintiff cannot recover damages that reasonable mitigation would have prevented.

*In use:* The landlord had to mitigate damages by trying to relet the apartment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mitigation_of_damages) · related: avoidable consequences, compensatory damages, consequential damages

### `nominal damages`

**Remedies**

A small sum awarded when a legal right was violated but no substantial loss was proved. Nominal damages can matter because they mark a legal wrong and may support other relief.

*In use:* The jury awarded one dollar in nominal damages for the trespass.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/nominal_damages) · related: compensatory damages, punitive damages, damages

### `permanent injunction`

**Remedies / Equity**

An injunction entered after final judgment that governs future conduct. It is not temporary case management; it is part of the final remedy.

*In use:* The final judgment included a permanent injunction against copying the software.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/permanent_injunction) · related: injunction, preliminary injunction, contempt

### `preliminary injunction`

**Remedies / Equity**

A provisional injunction issued before final judgment to preserve the status quo or prevent serious harm while the case is pending. Courts weigh likelihood of success, irreparable harm, equities, and public interest.

*In use:* The court granted a preliminary injunction until trial on the trade-secret claim.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/preliminary_injunction) · related: temporary restraining order, irreparable harm, injunction

### `punitive damages`

**Remedies**

Damages awarded to punish especially wrongful conduct and deter similar behavior. They are in addition to compensatory damages and are limited by due process.

*In use:* The jury awarded punitive damages after finding intentional fraud.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/punitive_damages) · related: compensatory damages, due process, malice

### `quantum meruit`

**Restitution / Contracts**

A restitution theory allowing recovery for the reasonable value of services provided when keeping the benefit without payment would be unjust. The phrase means recovery for as much as the work was worth.

*In use:* With no enforceable contract, the consultant pleaded quantum meruit.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quantum_meruit) · related: restitution, unjust enrichment, quasi-contract

### `rescission`

**Remedies / Contracts**

An equitable remedy that unwinds a transaction and returns the parties as nearly as possible to their pre-contract positions. It is common in fraud, mistake, duress, and some material breaches.

*In use:* The buyer sought rescission after discovering the seller's fraud.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/rescission) · related: restitution, reformation, contract

### `reformation`

**Remedies / Equity**

An equitable remedy rewriting a document so it reflects the parties' true agreement. It is often used when a writing contains a mistake or fails to capture agreed terms.

*In use:* The court ordered reformation because the deed misstated the boundary.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/reformation) · related: rescission, mistake, equitable remedy

### `reliance damages`

**Remedies / Contracts**

Damages that reimburse costs incurred in reasonable reliance on a promise or contract. They aim to put the injured party back where they were before relying.

*In use:* The startup sought reliance damages for money spent preparing to perform.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/reliance_damages) · related: expectation damages, promissory estoppel, restitution

### `restitution`

**Remedies**

A remedy aimed at restoring a benefit or preventing unjust enrichment. Restitution can require return of property, payment for value received, or disgorgement of gains.

*In use:* The court ordered restitution of the money obtained by mistake.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/restitution) · related: unjust enrichment, disgorgement, quantum meruit

### `specific performance`

**Remedies / Contracts**

An equitable order requiring a party to perform the promised act rather than pay damages. It is most common when the subject is unique, such as land or rare goods.

*In use:* The buyer sought specific performance of the contract to sell the farm.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/specific_performance) · related: adequate remedy at law, injunction, expectation damages
