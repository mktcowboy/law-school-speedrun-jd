# Legal Writing And Citation

Back to the [vocabulary overview](./README.md).

The working vocabulary of legal memos, briefs, case citations, citation signals, and source-checking.

### `authority`

**Legal Writing**

A source a lawyer or court relies on to support a legal proposition. Binding authority must be followed; persuasive authority may influence the court but does not control it.

*In use:* The brief led with binding authority from the state supreme court.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/authority) · related: precedent, citation, persuasive authority

### `block quote`

**Legal Writing**

A long quotation set off from the main text instead of run into a sentence. Use it sparingly; most legal writing is stronger when it paraphrases the rule and quotes only the essential words.

*In use:* The memo used a block quote for the statute's operative language.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: quotation, pincite, ellipsis

### `Bluebook`

**Legal Citation**

The dominant U.S. legal citation manual used by law schools, journals, and many courts. It governs citation form, signals, typefaces, abbreviations, and source order.

*In use:* The journal editor corrected the citation under the Bluebook rule for cases.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bluebook) · related: citation, case citation, citation signal

### `brief`

**Legal Writing / Advocacy**

A written argument submitted to a court. A brief frames the facts, states the legal issues, applies authority, and asks for a specific result.

*In use:* The appellant's brief argued that the trial court used the wrong standard.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/brief) · related: memorandum, argument, issue statement

### `case citation`

**Legal Citation**

The information that identifies a reported case: party names, volume, reporter, first page, court, and year. A full citation lets the reader find the exact decision.

*In use:* The case citation included the U.S. Reports volume and page.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: reporter, pincite, parallel citation

### `citation`

**Legal Citation**

A reference to a legal source such as a case, statute, regulation, rule, or secondary source. Citations let readers verify the proposition and assess the source's weight.

*In use:* Every rule sentence in the memo needed a citation.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: authority, Bluebook, pincite

### `citation signal`

**Legal Citation**

A word or phrase before a citation that tells the reader how the source relates to the proposition. Common signals include `see`, `see also`, `cf.`, `but see`, and `contra`.

*In use:* The writer used a citation signal because the case supported the point by analogy.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bluebook) · related: see, see also, contra

### `cite-checking`

**Legal Writing**

The process of confirming that every cited source exists, says what the writer claims, and is quoted or cited accurately. Good cite-checking catches both form errors and substance errors.

*In use:* Cite-checking revealed that the parenthetical overstated the holding.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: pincite, parenthetical, quotation

### `contra`

**Legal Citation**

A citation signal showing that the cited authority directly contradicts the proposition. It is stronger than `but see`, which usually indicates tension rather than direct opposition.

*In use:* The note used `contra` before a case reaching the opposite rule.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bluebook) · related: but see, citation signal, authority

### `e.g.`

**Legal Citation**

An abbreviation meaning "for example." In citations, it signals that the cited authorities are examples rather than an exhaustive list.

*In use:* The parenthetical began with `e.g.` because several other cases made the same point.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: citation signal, parenthetical, string citation

### `explanatory parenthetical`

**Legal Citation**

A short parenthetical after a citation explaining why the source matters. It should state the useful rule, fact, or holding rather than repeat the sentence it follows.

*In use:* The citation needed an explanatory parenthetical because the case was relevant by analogy.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: parenthetical, holding, pincite

### `holding`

**Reading Opinions / Legal Writing**

The court's actual ruling on the issue necessary to the judgment. Legal writing should use holdings for rules, not broad language detached from the result.

*In use:* The memo distinguished the holding from dicta in the same opinion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/holding) · related: dictum, precedent, rule statement

### `id.`

**Legal Citation**

A short-form citation meaning the same source as the immediately preceding citation. It saves space but only works when the reference is unambiguous.

*In use:* The second sentence cited the same page, so the writer used `id.`.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: supra, infra, short form

### `infra`

**Legal Citation**

A citation term directing the reader to material that appears later in the document. It is common in law review footnotes and less common in practice writing.

*In use:* The footnote used `infra` to point to the later discussion of remedies.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: supra, id., cross-reference

### `IRAC`

**Legal Writing**

A common law-school structure: issue, rule, application, conclusion. It is a training scaffold for organizing analysis, not a magic formula for every paragraph.

*In use:* The exam answer used IRAC to keep the negligence analysis readable.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_education) · related: issue statement, rule statement, conclusion

### `issue statement`

**Legal Writing**

A sentence or question identifying the legal problem the analysis will answer. Strong issue statements connect the rule to the legally important facts.

*In use:* The issue statement focused on whether the email created an enforceable offer.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/brief) · related: IRAC, rule statement, conclusion

### `memorandum`

**Legal Writing**

A legal memo is an internal or advisory document analyzing a legal question for a supervising lawyer, client, or court. It usually values objective prediction over advocacy.

*In use:* The associate wrote a memorandum assessing whether removal was proper.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/memorandum) · related: brief, issue statement, conclusion

### `parallel citation`

**Legal Citation**

A citation to the same case in more than one reporter. Parallel citations matter when a court rule requires a particular official or regional reporter.

*In use:* The state case required a parallel citation to both the official and regional reporters.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: reporter, case citation, pincite

### `parenthetical`

**Legal Citation**

Text in parentheses after a citation giving the reader context, such as a short quotation, procedural history, or explanatory phrase. The best parentheticals earn their space by clarifying relevance.

*In use:* The parenthetical explained that the case involved a nearly identical forum clause.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: explanatory parenthetical, citation signal, holding

### `pincite`

**Legal Citation**

A pinpoint citation to the exact page, section, paragraph, or line where the supporting material appears. It tells the reader where to check the proposition quickly.

*In use:* The court asked for a pincite to the sentence the brief quoted.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: case citation, quotation, reporter

### `quoting`

**Legal Citation**

A parenthetical signal showing that the cited source itself quotes another source. It helps track the chain of authority behind a borrowed statement.

*In use:* The citation used `(quoting ...)` because the court repeated language from an earlier case.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: parenthetical, quotation, authority

### `reporter`

**Legal Citation**

A published series of judicial decisions. Case citations identify the reporter so the reader can locate the opinion.

*In use:* The citation pointed to the Federal Reporter rather than the district court docket.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: case citation, parallel citation, slip opinion

### `rule statement`

**Legal Writing**

A sentence stating the governing legal rule before applying it to the facts. Good rule statements are specific enough to drive the analysis that follows.

*In use:* The rule statement separated duty, breach, causation, and damages.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/brief) · related: IRAC, holding, issue statement

### `see`

**Legal Citation**

A citation signal indicating that the cited authority clearly supports the proposition, though the connection may require a small inferential step. It is one of the most common support signals.

*In use:* The writer used `see` because the case applied the same rule to different facts.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bluebook) · related: citation signal, see also, authority

### `see also`

**Legal Citation**

A citation signal used for additional authority after stronger support has already been given. It is useful for reinforcing a point without making the first source carry all the work.

*In use:* The brief cited the controlling case first, then used `see also` for similar decisions.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bluebook) · related: see, citation signal, string citation

### `string citation`

**Legal Citation**

A sequence of multiple citations supporting one proposition. String citations should be curated, ordered, and explained when the relationship among sources is not obvious.

*In use:* The string citation showed that every circuit had adopted the same test.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: authority, citation signal, parenthetical

### `supra`

**Legal Citation**

A citation term directing the reader back to a source or discussion already cited earlier. It is common for books, articles, and internal cross-references, but not usually for cases.

*In use:* The footnote used `supra` to refer back to the treatise cited in note 4.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/citation) · related: infra, id., short form
