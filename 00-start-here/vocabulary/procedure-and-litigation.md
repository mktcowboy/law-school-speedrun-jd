# Procedure And Litigation

Back to the [vocabulary overview](./README.md).

How a case moves: pleadings, motions, discovery, jurisdiction, trial, judgment, and appeal.

### `adjudication`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's formal resolution of a dispute or issue. It can be a final judgment, or a narrower ruling that decides one part of the case.

*In use:* The agency's adjudication resolved the benefits dispute before judicial review began.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/adjudication) · related: judgment, hearing, due process

### `affidavit`

**General**

A written statement of facts sworn to under oath before someone authorized to administer oaths. Affidavits often support motions, warrants, and applications for emergency relief.

*In use:* The plaintiff attached an affidavit describing what the witness saw.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/affidavit) · related: declaration, perjury, motion

### `answer`

**Civil Procedure**

The defendant's formal written response to the complaint. It admits or denies allegations and raises defenses, counterclaims, or other responsive matters.

*In use:* The answer denied breach and pleaded the statute of limitations.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/answer) · related: complaint, pleading, affirmative defense

### `appeal`

**Appeals**

A request that a higher court review a lower court's judgment or order. Appeals usually focus on legal error, not a fresh trial of the facts.

*In use:* After final judgment, the defendant filed a timely appeal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/appeal) · related: appellant, appellee, remand

### `arbitration`

**Alternative Dispute Resolution**

A private dispute-resolution process in which one or more arbitrators decide the case. Arbitration may be binding if the parties agreed to treat the award as final.

*In use:* The court stayed the lawsuit and compelled arbitration under the contract.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/arbitration) · related: mediation, award, settlement

### `burden of proof`

**Evidence / Civil Procedure**

The obligation to prove a fact or claim. It can mean the burden of production, the burden of persuasion, or both.

*In use:* The plaintiff carried the burden of proof on causation and damages.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/burden_of_proof) · related: preponderance, clear and convincing evidence, reasonable doubt

### `case management order`

**Civil Procedure**

A court order setting deadlines and procedures for moving the case toward trial or resolution. It commonly covers discovery, amendments, motions, and settlement conferences.

*In use:* The case management order required expert reports by June.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/civil_procedure) · related: scheduling order, discovery, pretrial conference

### `cause of action`

**Civil Procedure**

A legally recognized claim that gives someone a right to seek relief in court. It is the legal theory plus the facts needed to make it actionable.

*In use:* The complaint stated a cause of action for negligence and one for breach of contract.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/cause_of_action) · related: claim, complaint, remedy

### `class action`

**Civil Procedure**

A lawsuit brought by one or more representatives on behalf of a larger group with similar claims. Certification requires, among other things, adequate representation and common issues.

*In use:* The consumers sought class action treatment for the same fee practice.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/class_action) · related: certification, numerosity, commonality

### `collateral estoppel`

**Civil Procedure**

Issue preclusion: once an issue has been actually litigated and necessarily decided, it generally cannot be relitigated later. It is narrower than claim preclusion.

*In use:* Collateral estoppel barred the defendant from disputing ownership again.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/collateral_estoppel) · related: res judicata, preclusion, final judgment

### `complaint`

**Civil Procedure**

The plaintiff's first pleading, which starts a civil lawsuit. It states the claims, the supporting facts, and the relief requested.

*In use:* The complaint alleged fraud and demanded a jury trial.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/complaint) · related: answer, pleading, cause of action

### `counterclaim`

**Civil Procedure**

A claim asserted by a defending party against an opposing party. Some counterclaims are compulsory because they arise from the same transaction or occurrence.

*In use:* The tenant answered and filed a counterclaim for breach of the lease.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/counterclaim) · related: answer, crossclaim, joinder

### `default judgment`

**Civil Procedure**

A judgment entered when a party fails to plead, defend, or otherwise respond as required. It gives the non-defaulting party relief without a trial on the merits.

*In use:* The court entered default judgment after the defendant ignored service.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/default_judgment) · related: service of process, judgment, set aside

### `demurrer`

**Civil Procedure**

A pleading challenge arguing that, even if the facts alleged are true, they do not state a legal claim. Federal practice replaced it with motions such as Rule 12(b)(6).

*In use:* The defendant demurred because the complaint alleged no duty.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/demurrer) · related: motion to dismiss, pleading, cause of action

### `deposition`

**Civil Procedure**

Sworn out-of-court testimony taken before trial, usually by question and answer. Depositions are used for discovery, impeachment, and sometimes trial testimony.

*In use:* At trial, counsel used the deposition to impeach the witness.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/deposition) · related: discovery, subpoena, witness

### `discovery`

**Civil Procedure**

The pretrial process for obtaining information from parties and nonparties. Common tools include depositions, interrogatories, document requests, and requests for admission.

*In use:* Discovery produced the email that changed the settlement posture.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/discovery) · related: deposition, interrogatories, request for admission

### `diversity jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

Federal subject-matter jurisdiction based on parties being citizens of different states and the amount in controversy exceeding the statutory threshold. It lets federal courts hear certain state-law disputes.

*In use:* Diversity jurisdiction existed because no plaintiff shared citizenship with any defendant.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/diversity_jurisdiction) · related: subject-matter jurisdiction, removal, Erie doctrine

### `Erie doctrine`

**Civil Procedure**

The rule that a federal court sitting in diversity applies federal procedural law but state substantive law. The hard exam question is whether a rule is procedural, substantive, or governed by a federal rule.

*In use:* Erie required the federal court to apply the state's statute of limitations.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/erie_doctrine) · related: diversity jurisdiction, choice of law, federal rules

### `federal question jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

Federal subject-matter jurisdiction over claims arising under the Constitution, federal statutes, or treaties. The federal issue usually must appear on the face of the well-pleaded complaint.

*In use:* The civil-rights claim supplied federal question jurisdiction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/federal_question_jurisdiction) · related: subject-matter jurisdiction, well-pleaded complaint, removal

### `forum non conveniens`

**Civil Procedure**

A doctrine allowing a court to dismiss a case when another forum is substantially more appropriate. It is used sparingly, especially when the alternative forum is abroad.

*In use:* The court dismissed on forum non conveniens grounds because the witnesses and evidence were overseas.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/forum_non_conveniens) · related: venue, transfer, jurisdiction

### `impleader`

**Civil Procedure**

A procedure by which a defending party brings in a third party who may be liable for all or part of the claim against the defending party. It is usually about indemnity or contribution.

*In use:* The contractor used impleader to bring the subcontractor into the case.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/impleader) · related: third-party complaint, joinder, contribution

### `intervention`

**Civil Procedure**

A procedure allowing a nonparty to join an existing lawsuit because the case may affect its interests. Intervention may be by right or by the court's permission.

*In use:* The insurer moved for intervention to protect its coverage position.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/intervention) · related: joinder, parties, standing

### `interrogatories`

**Civil Procedure**

Written discovery questions served on another party and answered under oath. They are useful for identifying witnesses, theories, and basic facts.

*In use:* The interrogatories asked the company to identify everyone involved in the decision.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/interrogatories) · related: discovery, deposition, request for admission

### `joinder`

**Civil Procedure**

The combining of claims or parties in one lawsuit. Joinder rules decide who may, should, or must be part of the same case.

*In use:* Joinder was proper because the claims arose from the same accident.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/joinder) · related: counterclaim, impleader, intervention

### `judgment`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's formal decision resolving the rights and obligations of the parties. A final judgment usually starts the clock for appeal.

*In use:* The clerk entered judgment for the plaintiff after the verdict.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/judgment) · related: verdict, final judgment, appeal

### `judgment as a matter of law`

**Civil Procedure**

A trial ruling that no reasonable jury could find for the opposing party on an issue. It takes the issue away from the jury because the evidence legally permits only one result.

*In use:* The defendant moved for judgment as a matter of law after the plaintiff rested.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/judgment_as_a_matter_of_law) · related: directed verdict, jury, summary judgment

### `jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's legal power to hear a case and bind the parties. Civil procedure usually separates subject-matter jurisdiction from personal jurisdiction.

*In use:* The court had jurisdiction over the dispute but transferred it for convenience.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/jurisdiction) · related: venue, personal jurisdiction, subject-matter jurisdiction

### `long-arm statute`

**Civil Procedure**

A state statute allowing its courts to exercise personal jurisdiction over out-of-state defendants in specified situations. The statute still must operate within constitutional due process limits.

*In use:* The long-arm statute reached the manufacturer because it shipped products into the forum.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/long-arm_statute) · related: personal jurisdiction, minimum contacts, due process

### `minimum contacts`

**Civil Procedure**

The constitutional baseline for personal jurisdiction over an out-of-state defendant. The defendant must have enough forum-related contacts that the suit is fair and foreseeable.

*In use:* A passive website did not create minimum contacts with the forum.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/minimum_contacts) · related: personal jurisdiction, long-arm statute, due process

### `motion`

**Civil Procedure**

A formal request asking the court to enter an order or ruling. The party making the request is the movant.

*In use:* The plaintiff filed a motion to compel better discovery responses.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/motion) · related: order, brief, hearing

### `motion in limine`

**Evidence / Trial**

A pretrial motion asking the court to admit or exclude evidence before it reaches the jury. It helps avoid unfair surprise or prejudicial material at trial.

*In use:* The court granted the motion in limine excluding the prior arrest.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/motion_in_limine) · related: evidence, objection, trial

### `motion to dismiss`

**Civil Procedure**

A motion asking the court to throw out a claim before discovery or trial, often because the complaint fails to state a claim. The court usually accepts well-pleaded facts as true at this stage.

*In use:* The court denied the motion to dismiss because the complaint plausibly alleged fraud.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/motion_to_dismiss) · related: demurrer, pleading, Rule 12(b)(6)

### `personal jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's power over a particular party. For an out-of-state defendant, the usual question is whether the defendant has sufficient minimum contacts with the forum.

*In use:* The defendant challenged personal jurisdiction before answering on the merits.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/personal_jurisdiction) · related: minimum contacts, long-arm statute, in personam

### `pleading`

**Civil Procedure**

A formal court document that states claims, defenses, or responses. In federal civil practice, the complaint and answer are the central pleadings.

*In use:* The judge read the pleading generously because the plaintiff was pro se.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pleading) · related: complaint, answer, amendment

### `preclusion`

**Civil Procedure**

The family of doctrines that prevent parties from relitigating claims or issues already decided. It includes claim preclusion and issue preclusion.

*In use:* Preclusion turned the first judgment into a defense against the second suit.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_judicata) · related: res judicata, collateral estoppel, final judgment

### `remand`

**Appeals / Civil Procedure**

To send a case back for further proceedings. An appellate court can remand to a trial court, and a federal court can remand an improperly removed case to state court.

*In use:* The appellate court reversed and remanded for a new damages trial.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/remand) · related: appeal, reverse, removal

### `removal`

**Civil Procedure**

A defendant's transfer of a case from state court to federal court when federal jurisdiction exists. Removal is common in diversity and federal-question cases.

*In use:* The defendant filed a notice of removal within thirty days of service.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/removal) · related: remand, diversity jurisdiction, federal question jurisdiction

### `request for admission`

**Civil Procedure**

A discovery request asking another party to admit or deny specific facts, document authenticity, or legal applications to fact. Admissions narrow what must be proved at trial.

*In use:* The unanswered request for admission established that the contract was signed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/request_for_admission) · related: discovery, interrogatories, stipulation

### `res judicata`

**Civil Procedure**

Claim preclusion: a final judgment on the merits generally bars the same parties from litigating the same claim again. It protects finality and prevents repeat lawsuits.

*In use:* Res judicata barred the second suit arising from the same sale.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_judicata) · related: collateral estoppel, preclusion, final judgment

### `sanctions`

**Civil Procedure**

Penalties a court imposes for litigation misconduct, frivolous filings, discovery abuse, or violation of court orders. Sanctions can include fees, evidentiary limits, dismissal, or default.

*In use:* The court imposed sanctions after the company destroyed responsive emails.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/sanction) · related: contempt, discovery, Rule 11

### `service of process`

**Civil Procedure**

The formal delivery of court papers that gives a defendant notice of the lawsuit and an opportunity to respond. Proper service is usually required before the court can proceed against a party.

*In use:* Service of process was defective because the summons went to the wrong address.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/service_of_process) · related: summons, complaint, default judgment

### `settlement`

**Civil Procedure**

An agreement resolving a dispute without a final adjudication on the merits. Settlement can happen before suit, during litigation, or even on appeal.

*In use:* The parties reached settlement after the court ruled on summary judgment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/settlement) · related: release, mediation, consent decree

### `standing`

**Civil Procedure / Constitutional Law**

The requirement that a plaintiff have a concrete stake in the dispute. Federal standing usually requires injury in fact, causation, and redressability.

*In use:* The court dismissed for lack of standing because the alleged injury was speculative.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/standing) · related: injury in fact, causation, redressability

### `statute of limitations`

**Civil Procedure**

A law setting the deadline for filing a claim. If the plaintiff waits too long, the defendant can raise the limitations period as a defense.

*In use:* The statute of limitations expired two years after the accident.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/statute_of_limitations) · related: tolling, laches, repose

### `subject-matter jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's power to hear the kind of case before it. Unlike personal jurisdiction, subject-matter jurisdiction cannot be waived by the parties.

*In use:* The federal court raised subject-matter jurisdiction on its own.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/subject_matter_jurisdiction) · related: jurisdiction, diversity jurisdiction, federal question jurisdiction

### `subpoena`

**Civil Procedure / Evidence**

A command requiring a person to appear, testify, produce documents, or permit inspection. Nonparties are often brought into discovery through subpoenas.

*In use:* The lawyer served a subpoena on the bank for account records.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/subpoena) · related: deposition, discovery, witness

### `summary judgment`

**Civil Procedure**

A judgment entered without trial when no genuine dispute of material fact exists and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. It tests whether a trial is legally necessary.

*In use:* The court granted summary judgment because the video resolved the key fact.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/summary_judgment) · related: motion, material fact, judgment

### `venue`

**Civil Procedure**

The proper geographic court for a case. Venue asks where within a court system a suit should be heard, while jurisdiction asks whether the court has power.

*In use:* Venue was proper in the district where the contract was performed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/venue) · related: forum, transfer, jurisdiction

### `verdict`

**Trial**

A jury's formal finding at the end of trial. The verdict may decide liability, damages, guilt, or specific factual questions.

*In use:* The jury returned a verdict for the plaintiff on negligence.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/verdict) · related: jury, judgment, trial

### `voir dire`

**Trial**

Preliminary questioning used to screen jurors for bias or, in some contexts, to test a witness's qualifications. In jury selection, it sets up challenges for cause and peremptory strikes.

*In use:* During voir dire, three potential jurors disclosed ties to the defendant.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/voir_dire) · related: jury selection, challenge for cause, peremptory challenge
