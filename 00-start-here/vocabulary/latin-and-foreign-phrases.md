# Latin And Foreign Phrases

Back to the [vocabulary overview](./README.md) · [start-here dashboard](../README.md) · [subject index](../../SUBJECTS.md).

Borrowed phrases show up everywhere in casebooks. Learn what they do in context: some name doctrines, some label procedural moves, and some are just lawyer shorthand.

### `actus reus`

**Criminal Law** · lit. "guilty act"

The physical element of a crime: a voluntary act, omission, or legally relevant conduct. It usually pairs with `mens rea`, because criminal liability normally requires both a guilty act and a guilty mind.

*In use:* The prosecution proved intent but still had to prove the actus reus.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/actus_reus) · related: mens rea, omission

### `ad hoc`

**General** · lit. "to this"

Created or done for a particular purpose, often temporarily. Courts use it to describe case-specific decisions, committees, or arrangements that are not part of a standing rule.

*In use:* The agency made an ad hoc exception for that proceeding.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ad_hoc) · related: discretion, case-by-case

### `ad litem`

**Civil Procedure / Family Law** · lit. "for the suit"

For purposes of the litigation. You will most often see it in `guardian ad litem`, a person appointed to protect a child or incompetent person's interests in a case.

*In use:* The court appointed a guardian ad litem before approving the settlement.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/guardian_ad_litem) · related: capacity, representative

### `amicus curiae`

**Appeals** · lit. "friend of the court"

A non-party who files a brief, usually with permission, to offer information or argument that may help the court. Amicus briefs are especially common in appellate and Supreme Court cases.

*In use:* Several civil-rights groups filed amicus curiae briefs.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/amicus_curiae) · related: brief, appellate advocacy

### `bona fide`

**General** · lit. "in good faith"

Genuine, honest, and without fraud or notice of a defect. The phrase often matters in property and commercial law, as in a bona fide purchaser.

*In use:* A bona fide purchaser may take priority over an earlier unrecorded interest.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bona_fide_purchaser) · related: good faith, notice

### `caveat emptor`

**Property / Contracts** · lit. "let the buyer beware"

The old common-law rule that a buyer bears the risk of a purchase's quality unless the seller made a warranty or committed fraud. Modern warranty and consumer-protection rules limit it.

*In use:* Caveat emptor gave way to the implied warranty of habitability.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/caveat_emptor) · related: warranty, fraud

### `certiorari`

**Appeals** · lit. "to be more fully informed"

A discretionary writ by which a higher court, especially the U.S. Supreme Court, agrees to review a lower court's decision. "Cert denied" means the lower-court judgment stays in place without Supreme Court review.

*In use:* The Court granted certiorari to resolve the circuit split.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/certiorari) · related: writ, appeal

### `contra proferentem`

**Contracts** · lit. "against the offeror"

The rule that an ambiguous contract term is construed against the party who drafted it. It often appears with insurance policies and form contracts.

*In use:* The ambiguous exclusion was read against the insurer under contra proferentem.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/contra_proferentem) · related: ambiguity, interpretation

### `coram nobis`

**Criminal Procedure / Appeals** · lit. "before us"

A rare writ used to correct a fundamental error after the ordinary time for appeal has passed, often when the person is no longer in custody. It is an extraordinary remedy, not a second appeal.

*In use:* He sought coram nobis relief after the conviction carried immigration consequences.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/habeas_corpus) · related: habeas corpus, collateral review

### `corpus delicti`

**Criminal Law** · lit. "body of the crime"

The basic fact that a crime occurred, apart from who committed it. Some doctrines require proof beyond a confession before a conviction can rest on the defendant's statement.

*In use:* The burned building supplied evidence of the corpus delicti for arson.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/corpus_delicti) · related: confession, proof

### `cy pres`

**Trusts / Remedies** · French "as near as"

An equitable doctrine that lets a court modify a charitable trust or settlement distribution so it comes as close as possible to the original purpose when literal compliance is impracticable.

*In use:* The court used cy pres to redirect the charity funds to a similar purpose.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/cy_pres) · related: charitable trust, equity

### `de facto`

**General** · lit. "in fact"

Existing in reality, even if not formally recognized by law. Contrast `de jure`, which means legally recognized.

*In use:* The policy created a de facto ban even without saying so.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/de_facto) · related: de jure, practical effect

### `de jure`

**General** · lit. "by law"

Existing as a matter of legal right or formal legal status. It contrasts with `de facto`, which describes what happens in practice.

*In use:* The statute imposed de jure segregation before it was struck down.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/de_jure) · related: de facto, legal status

### `de minimis`

**General** · lit. "about minimal things"

Too small or trivial for the law to concern itself with. Courts use it when a technical violation or injury is not legally meaningful enough to justify relief.

*In use:* The court treated the few cents of underpayment as de minimis.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/de_minimis) · related: harmless error, materiality

### `de novo`

**Appeals / Civil Procedure** · lit. "anew"

A fresh review with no deference to the decision below. Appellate courts usually review pure questions of law de novo.

*In use:* Contract interpretation was reviewed de novo.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/de_novo) · related: standard of review, question of law

### `dictum`

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "something said"

A statement in an opinion that is not necessary to the result. Dictum can be persuasive, but it is not the binding holding.

*In use:* The plaintiff relied on dictum from an earlier case.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/dictum) · related: holding, ratio decidendi

### `duces tecum`

**Civil Procedure / Evidence** · lit. "you shall bring with you"

Used in subpoenas that require a person to bring documents, records, or other evidence. The fuller phrase is `subpoena duces tecum`.

*In use:* The subpoena duces tecum demanded the company's payroll records.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/subpoena_duces_tecum) · related: subpoena, discovery

### `en banc`

**Appeals** · French "on the bench"

Heard by all active judges of an appellate court, or by a larger authorized group, rather than a smaller panel. En banc review is usually reserved for unusually important questions or conflicts within the court.

*In use:* The circuit reheard the appeal en banc.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/en_banc) · related: panel, rehearing

### `ex parte`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "from one side"

Done for or by one party without the other side present. Because the adversary is absent, ex parte relief is usually limited and closely supervised.

*In use:* The judge issued an ex parte temporary restraining order.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ex_parte) · related: notice, hearing

### `ex post facto`

**Constitutional / Criminal Law** · lit. "after the fact"

Retroactively criminalizing conduct or increasing punishment after the act occurred. The Constitution bars ex post facto criminal laws.

*In use:* The new punishment could not apply retroactively without raising an ex post facto problem.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ex_post_facto) · related: retroactivity, due process

### `forum non conveniens`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "an inconvenient forum"

A doctrine allowing a court to dismiss a case because another court is substantially more convenient and appropriate. It often appears in transnational or multi-state disputes.

*In use:* The defendant sought dismissal on forum non conveniens grounds.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/forum_non_conveniens) · related: venue, transfer

### `habeas corpus`

**Criminal / Civil Procedure** · lit. "that you have the body"

A writ requiring the government to justify a person's detention before a court. It is a central vehicle for challenging unlawful custody.

*In use:* The prisoner filed a habeas corpus petition after direct appeal failed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/habeas_corpus) · related: custody, collateral review

### `in camera`

**Procedure / Evidence** · lit. "in chambers"

Private judicial review outside public view, often to decide privilege, confidentiality, or sensitive evidence questions. The phrase does not mean the judge is deciding secretly without legal standards.

*In use:* The judge reviewed the disputed emails in camera.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_camera) · related: privilege, confidentiality

### `in forma pauperis`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "in the manner of a poor person"

Permission to proceed in court without paying ordinary filing fees because the party cannot afford them. It is common in prisoner and civil-rights litigation.

*In use:* The court granted leave to proceed in forma pauperis.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_forma_pauperis) · related: filing fee, access to courts

### `in limine`

**Evidence / Trial** · lit. "at the threshold"

A pretrial request asking the court to admit or exclude evidence before it comes up in front of the jury. Motions in limine help avoid unfair prejudice or trial disruption.

*In use:* The plaintiff moved in limine to exclude the prior conviction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_limine) · related: evidence, motion

### `in loco parentis`

**Family / Education Law** · lit. "in the place of a parent"

Describes someone or some institution acting with parental responsibilities or authority. Schools and guardianship disputes often use the phrase.

*In use:* The school argued it acted in loco parentis during the field trip.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_loco_parentis) · related: parent, custody

### `in pari delicto`

**Equity / Torts** · lit. "in equal fault"

An equitable defense that can bar a plaintiff who participated in the same wrongdoing as the defendant. The core idea is that a court should not help one wrongdoer recover from another for the shared misconduct.

*In use:* The fraud claim was barred because the parties were in pari delicto.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_pari_delicto) · related: unclean hands, equity

### `in personam`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "against the person"

Jurisdiction or a judgment directed at a particular person and capable of imposing personal liability. It contrasts with `in rem`, which is directed at property.

*In use:* Minimum contacts supported in personam jurisdiction over the defendant.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_personam) · related: personal jurisdiction, in rem

### `in rem`

**Civil Procedure / Property** · lit. "against the thing"

Jurisdiction or a judgment directed at property itself. An in rem judgment determines interests in the property against the world.

*In use:* The forfeiture action proceeded in rem against the vessel.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_rem) · related: property, in personam

### `inter alia`

**General** · lit. "among other things"

Signals that the listed items are examples, not an exhaustive list. It is common in opinions, briefs, and pleadings.

*In use:* The complaint alleged, inter alia, fraud and breach of fiduciary duty.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/inter_alia) · related: pleading, legal writing

### `inter vivos`

**Property / Trusts & Estates** · lit. "between the living"

Made or transferred during a person's lifetime, rather than at death. The phrase often appears in trust and gift law.

*In use:* She created an inter vivos trust to manage the property during her life.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/inter_vivos) · related: trust, gift

### `ipso facto`

**General** · lit. "by the fact itself"

By that very fact or by the act alone. In legal writing it often marks a conclusion said to follow automatically from a fact.

*In use:* A burdensome regulation is not ipso facto unconstitutional.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ipso_facto) · related: inference, legal conclusion

### `laches`

**Equity** · Old French "slackness"

An equitable defense based on unreasonable delay that prejudices the opposing party. It is not just lateness; the delay must make the case unfair.

*In use:* The defendant raised laches after the plaintiff waited years to sue.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/laches) · related: equity, statute of limitations

### `lis pendens`

**Property / Civil Procedure** · lit. "pending suit"

A notice that litigation involving real property is pending. It warns potential buyers or lenders that the lawsuit may affect title.

*In use:* The buyer discovered a lis pendens recorded against the land.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/lis_pendens) · related: notice, title

### `mandamus`

**Courts / Remedies** · lit. "we command"

An extraordinary writ ordering a public official or lower court to perform a nondiscretionary duty. It is not a substitute for ordinary appeal.

*In use:* The petitioner sought mandamus to compel the agency to act.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mandamus) · related: writ, ministerial duty

### `mens rea`

**Criminal Law** · lit. "guilty mind"

The mental element of a crime, such as purpose, knowledge, recklessness, or negligence. It pairs with `actus reus`.

*In use:* The word "knowingly" supplied the required mens rea.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mens_rea) · related: actus reus, scienter

### `nolle prosequi`

**Criminal Procedure** · lit. "to be unwilling to prosecute"

A prosecutor's formal decision to drop charges or stop pursuing a case. The effect can depend on timing and local rules.

*In use:* The state entered a nolle prosequi after the key witness recanted.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/nolle_prosequi) · related: dismissal, prosecutor

### `nolo contendere`

**Criminal Law** · lit. "I do not wish to contest"

A plea that accepts conviction without admitting guilt. It can limit the plea's use as an admission in later civil litigation.

*In use:* The defendant pleaded nolo contendere to the misdemeanor charge.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/nolo_contendere) · related: plea, guilty plea

### `nunc pro tunc`

**Courts / Civil Procedure** · lit. "now for then"

An order entered now but treated as effective on an earlier date, usually to correct the record so it reflects what should have happened. It cannot be used to rewrite history for a new substantive ruling.

*In use:* The court entered a nunc pro tunc order correcting the judgment date.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/nunc_pro_tunc) · related: judgment, clerical error

### `parens patriae`

**Constitutional / Family Law** · lit. "parent of the country"

The state's authority to protect people who cannot protect themselves, or to sue to protect sovereign or quasi-sovereign interests. It appears in child welfare, mental health, and state enforcement cases.

*In use:* The state sued as parens patriae on behalf of injured residents.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/parens_patriae) · related: state power, custody

### `per curiam`

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "by the court"

An opinion issued in the name of the court rather than a named judge. It may be short and unsigned, but it is still a court opinion.

*In use:* The Court reversed in a brief per curiam opinion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/per_curiam) · related: opinion, appellate court

### `per se`

**General** · lit. "by itself"

In itself or automatically. A per se rule applies without a detailed case-by-case balancing inquiry.

*In use:* Horizontal price fixing is treated as unlawful per se.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/per_se) · related: rule of reason, negligence per se

### `prima facie`

**General** · lit. "at first appearance"

Enough on its face to establish a claim, defense, or fact unless rebutted. A prima facie case usually shifts the burden of production to the other side.

*In use:* The plaintiff made a prima facie showing of discrimination.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/prima_facie) · related: burden of proof, rebuttal

### `pro bono`

**Professional Responsibility** · lit. "for the good"

Legal work performed without charge for the public good. The fuller phrase is `pro bono publico`.

*In use:* The firm handled the asylum appeal pro bono.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pro_bono) · related: access to justice, legal services

### `pro hac vice`

**Legal Profession** · lit. "for this occasion"

Permission for an out-of-state lawyer to appear in a particular case even though the lawyer is not admitted generally in that court. Local counsel is often required.

*In use:* The court admitted trial counsel pro hac vice.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pro_hac_vice) · related: bar admission, counsel

### `pro se`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "for oneself"

Representing oneself in court without a lawyer. Courts often construe pro se filings generously, but self-represented parties still must follow procedural rules.

*In use:* The tenant filed a pro se answer.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pro_se) · related: counsel, self-representation

### `quantum meruit`

**Contracts / Restitution** · lit. "as much as deserved"

A restitution theory allowing recovery for the reasonable value of services when no enforceable contract controls and nonpayment would unjustly enrich the recipient.

*In use:* The contractor sought quantum meruit for the extra work.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quantum_meruit) · related: restitution, unjust enrichment

### `quasi contract`

**Contracts / Restitution** · lit. "as if a contract"

An obligation imposed by law to prevent unjust enrichment, even though the parties did not make an enforceable contract. It is a restitution remedy, not a real contract.

*In use:* The court used quasi contract to require payment for emergency services.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quasi_contract) · related: unjust enrichment, quantum meruit

### `qui tam`

**Civil Procedure / Statutory Enforcement** · lit. "who as well"

A lawsuit brought by a private relator on the government's behalf, often under the False Claims Act. The relator may receive part of the recovery.

*In use:* The whistleblower filed a qui tam action alleging Medicare fraud.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/qui_tam) · related: relator, False Claims Act

### `quid pro quo`

**Contracts / Criminal Law** · lit. "this for that"

An exchange of one thing for another. It can describe consideration in contract law or an improper exchange in bribery and harassment contexts.

*In use:* The email suggested a quid pro quo for the official favor.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quid_pro_quo) · related: consideration, bribery

### `ratio decidendi`

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "reason for deciding"

The rule or reasoning necessary to the court's decision. It is the binding core of a case, contrasted with dictum.

*In use:* The ratio decidendi turned on the statute's definition of employee.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ratio_decidendi) · related: holding, dictum

### `res ipsa loquitur`

**Torts** · lit. "the thing speaks for itself"

A way to infer negligence from the kind of accident that ordinarily would not happen without negligence. It is most useful when the defendant controlled the instrumentality and the plaintiff lacks direct proof.

*In use:* The patient invoked res ipsa loquitur after the surgical sponge was left inside.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_ipsa_loquitur) · related: negligence, circumstantial evidence

### `res judicata`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "a matter judged"

Claim preclusion: a final judgment on the merits bars the same parties from relitigating the same claim. It protects finality and prevents repetitive litigation.

*In use:* Res judicata barred the second suit over the same contract.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_judicata) · related: collateral estoppel, final judgment

### `respondeat superior`

**Torts / Agency** · lit. "let the superior answer"

The rule that an employer can be vicariously liable for an employee's torts committed within the scope of employment. It does not require the employer's personal fault.

*In use:* The delivery company was liable under respondeat superior.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/respondeat_superior) · related: vicarious liability, agency

### `scienter`

**Criminal / Securities Law** · lit. "knowingly"

Knowledge of wrongdoing or an intent to deceive. It is a key mental-state requirement in fraud and securities-fraud claims.

*In use:* The complaint failed because it did not plead scienter with particularity.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/scienter) · related: mens rea, fraud

### `stare decisis`

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "to stand by things decided"

The doctrine that courts should follow precedent. It can operate vertically, binding lower courts to higher courts, and horizontally, guiding a court's treatment of its own past decisions.

*In use:* Stare decisis weighed against overruling the old case.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/stare_decisis) · related: precedent, holding

### `sua sponte`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "of its own accord"

An action taken by a court on its own initiative, without a party's request. Courts often raise jurisdictional defects sua sponte.

*In use:* The court dismissed the case sua sponte for lack of jurisdiction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/sua_sponte) · related: jurisdiction, motion

### `subpoena`

**Civil Procedure / Evidence** · lit. "under penalty"

A command requiring someone to testify, produce evidence, or both. Ignoring a valid subpoena can lead to contempt.

*In use:* The witness appeared only after receiving a subpoena.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/subpoena) · related: witness, discovery

### `subpoena duces tecum`

**Civil Procedure / Evidence** · lit. "under penalty, you shall bring with you"

A subpoena requiring the recipient to produce documents or things. It may also require testimony, depending on the command.

*In use:* The subpoena duces tecum requested bank statements and ledgers.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/subpoena_duces_tecum) · related: subpoena, document request

### `sui generis`

**General** · lit. "of its own kind"

Unique or in a class by itself. Courts use it when a rule, right, or legal category does not fit ordinary classifications.

*In use:* The court described tribal sovereignty as sui generis.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/sui_generis) · related: classification, unique

### `ultra vires`

**Business Associations / Administrative Law** · lit. "beyond the powers"

Outside the legal authority of a corporation, officer, agency, or other actor. The question is whether the actor had power to do the act at all.

*In use:* The shareholders challenged the guarantee as ultra vires.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ultra_vires) · related: authority, agency

### `voir dire`

**Trial / Evidence** · French "to speak the truth"

Preliminary questioning, most often of prospective jurors to uncover bias. It can also refer to questioning a witness about qualifications or admissibility before testimony.

*In use:* During voir dire, the judge excused two jurors for cause.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/voir_dire) · related: jury selection, challenge for cause
