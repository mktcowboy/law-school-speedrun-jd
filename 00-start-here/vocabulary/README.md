# Vocabulary

Back to the [start-here dashboard](../README.md) · [subject index](../../SUBJECTS.md).

The curriculum's legal vocabulary — the Latin, the jargon, and the terms of art that fill casebooks, opinions, and exams. It is a cross-subject reference: use it while you read, not as a list to memorize cold. Every entry links to Cornell LII [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex) for a fuller treatment.

## How To Use It

- **Look up, don't cram.** When a term stops you mid-case, define it here, then keep reading. Retention comes from seeing terms in context, not from flashcards alone.
- **Tie each term to something you have read.** A definition sticks once you can name the case or rule where you met it; note that link in your own outline.
- **Learn the pairs.** Many terms only make sense against their opposite — `actus reus` / `mens rea`, `in personam` / `in rem`, `res judicata` / `collateral estoppel`, `holding` / `dictum`.
- **Move from phrase to primary source.** Follow the Wex link whenever the one-line definition is not enough.

## Entry Format

Every entry across every file follows the same shape:

```
### `term`

**Subject** · lit. "literal translation" (Latin or French only)

A plain-English definition in one or two sentences.

*In use:* A short sentence showing the term in a real context.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/term) · related: nearby terms
```

## Adding A Term

- Put the term in the right file — a cross-cutting category, or the subject in [`doctrine/`](./doctrine/README.md) where a student first meets it.
- Alphabetize within its section. Two-sentence definition; plain English first, then the precision.
- Link the matching Wex entry at `https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/<term_with_underscores>`. **Verify the link resolves before committing** — Wex uses the canonical term, so descriptive compound slugs often 404 (use `ex_post_facto`, not `ex_post_facto_law`; `in_rem`, not `in_rem_jurisdiction`). When a term has no page, link the closest broader entry, or omit the `See:` line for writing/citation terms that Wex does not cover.
- Prefer high-frequency terms — words you will actually meet in casebooks, opinions, and exams — over exotic ones.
