# Reading And Analyzing Opinions

Back to the [vocabulary overview](./README.md) · [start-here dashboard](../README.md) · [subject index](../../SUBJECTS.md).

Opinion vocabulary helps you separate what happened, what the court decided, and what later courts must do with it. Use these terms while briefing cases and while turning cases into rules for exams.

### `abuse of discretion`

**Appeals**

A deferential standard of review for decisions committed to a lower court's judgment, such as many evidentiary and case-management rulings. The appellant must show more than disagreement; the decision must fall outside the range of permissible choices.

*In use:* The appellate court reviewed the denial of a continuance for abuse of discretion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/abuse_of_discretion) · related: standard of review, clear error

### `affirmed`

**Appeals**

The appellate court leaves the lower court's judgment in place. It may affirm because the lower court was right, or because any error did not justify changing the result.

*In use:* The judgment was affirmed despite a minor evidentiary error.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/appeal) · related: reverse, remand

### `appeal`

**Appeals**

A request that a higher court review a lower court's judgment or order. Appeals usually focus on legal error, not a fresh trial of every fact.

*In use:* The defendant filed an appeal after final judgment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/appeal) · related: appellant, standard of review

### `appellant`

**Appeals**

The party asking the appellate court to change the decision below. In many cases, the appellant carries the burden of showing reversible error.

*In use:* The appellant argued that the jury instruction misstated the law.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/appellant) · related: appellee, appeal

### `appellee`

**Appeals**

The party defending the judgment or order on appeal. The appellee usually argues that the decision below should be affirmed.

*In use:* The appellee urged the court to affirm on an alternative ground.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/appellee) · related: appellant, respondent

### `binding authority`

**Reading Opinions**

Law that a court must follow, such as a controlling statute or precedent from a higher court in the same jurisdiction. It differs from persuasive authority, which may influence but does not bind.

*In use:* A state trial court treated the state supreme court's decision as binding authority.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/precedent) · related: mandatory authority, persuasive authority

### `case of first impression`

**Reading Opinions**

A case presenting a legal question that the deciding court has not previously resolved. Courts in first-impression cases often look to text, policy, analogous precedent, and other jurisdictions.

*In use:* The court called the privacy issue a case of first impression in the state.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/case_of_first_impression) · related: precedent, persuasive authority

### `certiorari`

**Appeals**

Discretionary review by a higher court, especially the U.S. Supreme Court. When certiorari is denied, the lower court's decision remains but the denial is not an endorsement of its reasoning.

*In use:* The Supreme Court denied certiorari, ending the appeal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/certiorari) · related: appeal, circuit split

### `circuit split`

**Appeals**

A disagreement among federal courts of appeals on the same legal question. Circuit splits often make Supreme Court review more likely because federal law is being applied differently across regions.

*In use:* The opinion noted a circuit split over the statute's reach.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/circuit_split) · related: certiorari, precedent

### `clear error`

**Appeals**

A deferential standard used for factual findings, especially by judges. The reviewing court asks whether it is left with a firm conviction that a mistake was made.

*In use:* The trial court's factual finding survived clear-error review.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/standard_of_review) · related: question of fact, abuse of discretion

### `concurring opinion`

**Reading Opinions**

An opinion by a judge who agrees with the judgment but writes separately. A concurrence may offer a different rationale, narrow the holding, or signal how future cases might come out.

*In use:* The concurring opinion supplied the fifth vote but used narrower reasoning.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/concurring_opinion) · related: majority opinion, dissenting opinion

### `de novo review`

**Appeals**

Fresh appellate review with no deference to the lower court's answer. Courts use it most often for questions of law.

*In use:* The court applied de novo review to the statute's meaning.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/de_novo) · related: standard of review, question of law

### `dictum`

**Reading Opinions**

A statement in an opinion that is not necessary to the court's decision. Dictum can be useful and persuasive, but later courts are not bound by it.

*In use:* The broad sentence about online searches was dictum.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/dictum) · related: holding, ratio decidendi

### `dissenting opinion`

**Reading Opinions**

An opinion by a judge who disagrees with the court's judgment. Dissents are not law, but they can shape later doctrine and exam analysis.

*In use:* The dissenting opinion warned that the rule would be hard to administer.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/dissenting_opinion) · related: majority opinion, concurring opinion

### `distinguish`

**Reading Opinions**

To explain why an earlier case does not control because its facts, rule, or legal setting are materially different. Distinguishing is how courts follow precedent without extending it.

*In use:* The court distinguished the landlord case because this lease involved commercial property.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/precedent) · related: precedent, holding

### `en banc`

**Appeals**

Review by all active judges of an appellate court, or by a larger authorized group, rather than the ordinary panel. En banc decisions can overrule circuit precedent that a panel could not.

*In use:* The court took the case en banc to reconsider its prior rule.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/en_banc) · related: panel, rehearing

### `facts`

**Reading Opinions**

The legally relevant events and circumstances that frame the dispute. Good case reading separates background facts from the facts the court actually uses in its reasoning.

*In use:* The holding depends on the facts that the officer entered without a warrant.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/finding_of_fact) · related: procedural history, issue

### `holding`

**Reading Opinions**

The court's answer to the legal issue, tied to the material facts and necessary to the judgment. The holding is the part of the case later courts treat as precedent.

*In use:* The holding was limited to searches of cell phones incident to arrest.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/holding) · related: dictum, ratio decidendi

### `issue`

**Reading Opinions**

The legal question the court must answer to decide the case. A useful issue statement includes the relevant law and the facts that make the question matter.

*In use:* The issue was whether the promise was supported by consideration.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/issue) · related: holding, question of law

### `judgment`

**Appeals / Civil Procedure**

The court's official resolution of the case or a claim, such as entering damages, dismissal, or equitable relief. The opinion explains reasons; the judgment states the legal result.

*In use:* The appellate court affirmed the judgment but rejected part of the reasoning.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/judgment) · related: disposition, final judgment

### `majority opinion`

**Reading Opinions**

An opinion joined by more than half of the judges deciding the case. It usually supplies the controlling reasoning and holding.

*In use:* The majority opinion adopted a bright-line rule for police searches.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/opinion) · related: concurring opinion, dissenting opinion

### `opinion`

**Reading Opinions**

The court's written explanation of its decision. It may include facts, procedural history, reasoning, and separate opinions from judges who concur or dissent.

*In use:* The opinion reversed the dismissal and explained the pleading standard.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/opinion) · related: judgment, holding

### `overrule`

**Reading Opinions**

To reject a prior precedent so it no longer controls. A lower court cannot overrule a higher court; it must follow controlling precedent until the higher court changes it.

*In use:* The state supreme court overruled its earlier privity requirement.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/overrule) · related: precedent, stare decisis

### `persuasive authority`

**Reading Opinions**

Law or reasoning that a court may consider but does not have to follow. Examples include sister-state cases, lower-court opinions, treatises, and dissents.

*In use:* The court treated the California decision as persuasive authority.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/persuasive_authority) · related: binding authority, secondary authority

### `plurality opinion`

**Reading Opinions**

An opinion that receives more votes than any other opinion but not a majority of the court. Identifying the controlling rule in plurality cases can be tricky.

*In use:* Students struggled to extract a rule from the plurality opinion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/opinion) · related: majority opinion, concurrence

### `precedent`

**Reading Opinions**

An earlier decision used as authority in a later case. Whether precedent binds depends on court hierarchy, jurisdiction, and whether the earlier case's holding fits the new facts.

*In use:* The trial court followed precedent from the state supreme court.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/precedent) · related: stare decisis, holding

### `procedural history`

**Reading Opinions**

The path the case took before reaching the present court: pleadings, motions, trial, verdict, judgment, and prior appeals. It tells you what question is actually before the court now.

*In use:* The procedural history showed that only the damages issue was on appeal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/civil_procedure) · related: appeal, posture

### `question of fact`

**Reading Opinions**

A dispute about what happened or what inference should be drawn from evidence. Appellate courts usually defer to factfinders on these questions.

*In use:* Whether the driver ran the red light was a question of fact.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/question_of_fact) · related: factfinder, clear error

### `question of law`

**Reading Opinions**

A dispute about what the law means or how a legal rule should be stated. Appellate courts usually review questions of law de novo.

*In use:* The meaning of the statute was a question of law.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/question_of_law) · related: de novo review, standard of review

### `rationale`

**Reading Opinions**

The court's reason for reaching its result. A case can have the same judgment but different possible rationales, which matters when predicting how far the rule extends.

*In use:* The concurrence agreed with the result but rejected the majority's rationale.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/holding) · related: reasoning, ratio decidendi

### `remand`

**Appeals**

To send a case back to a lower court for further proceedings. Remand often follows reversal or vacatur, but the lower court's next steps depend on the appellate mandate.

*In use:* The court reversed and remanded for a new trial.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/remand) · related: reverse, mandate

### `reverse`

**Appeals**

To overturn the lower court's judgment or ruling. A reversal may end the case or send it back for more proceedings under the appellate court's instructions.

*In use:* The appellate court reversed the grant of summary judgment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/reversal) · related: affirm, remand

### `standard of review`

**Appeals**

The level of deference an appellate court gives to the decision below. Spotting the standard of review often tells you how hard the appeal will be to win.

*In use:* The outcome turned on the standard of review, not just the underlying rule.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/standard_of_review) · related: de novo review, abuse of discretion

### `stare decisis`

**Reading Opinions**

The doctrine of following precedent. It gives legal rules stability while still leaving room for courts to distinguish or overrule past cases.

*In use:* Stare decisis carried special force because parties had relied on the rule.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/stare_decisis) · related: precedent, overrule

### `syllabus`

**Reading Opinions**

A summary that appears before some reported opinions, especially Supreme Court opinions. It helps orient the reader, but it is not the opinion of the court.

*In use:* The syllabus was useful, but the exam answer needed the actual holding.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/opinion) · related: headnote, opinion

### `vacate`

**Appeals / Civil Procedure**

To set aside or nullify a judgment or order. A vacated decision usually loses legal effect as a judgment and may also lose precedential force.

*In use:* The appellate court vacated the injunction and remanded.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/vacate) · related: remand, judgment
