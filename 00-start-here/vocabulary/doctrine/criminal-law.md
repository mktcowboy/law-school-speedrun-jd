# Criminal Law Vocabulary

Back to the [doctrine vocabulary index](./README.md) · [vocabulary overview](../README.md).

Core terms for the 1L criminal law course: elements, mental states, inchoate crimes, homicide, defenses, and grading.

### `actus reus`

**Criminal Law**

The physical element of a crime: a voluntary act, or an omission when the law imposes a duty to act. Criminal liability usually requires both actus reus and mens rea.

*In use:* The prosecution proved intent, but still had to show an actus reus.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/actus_reus) · related: mens rea, omission

### `accomplice liability`

**Criminal Law**

Liability for intentionally helping, encouraging, or facilitating another person's crime. The accomplice is usually punished as a principal for the offense aided.

*In use:* Driving the getaway car supported accomplice liability for the robbery.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/accomplice) · related: conspiracy, principal

### `arson`

**Criminal Law**

The malicious burning of property. At common law it focused on another person's dwelling; modern statutes often cover many kinds of buildings and property.

*In use:* Setting fire to the occupied building made the charge arson, not mere vandalism.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/arson) · related: malice, property crime

### `attempt`

**Criminal Law**

An inchoate crime committed when a person intends to commit an offense and takes a substantial step toward completing it. Mere preparation is not enough.

*In use:* Buying tools was preparation, but breaking the lock was enough for attempt.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/attempt) · related: solicitation, conspiracy

### `burglary`

**Criminal Law**

At common law, breaking and entering another's dwelling at night with intent to commit a felony inside. Modern statutes often broaden the time, place, and target offense.

*In use:* Entering the store after closing with intent to steal supported burglary.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/burglary) · related: larceny, robbery

### `causation`

**Criminal Law**

Causation connects the defendant's conduct to a prohibited result, such as death in homicide. The usual questions are factual cause and legal or proximate cause.

*In use:* The intervening medical error raised a causation issue in the homicide charge.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/proximate_cause) · related: actus reus, result crime

### `conspiracy`

**Criminal Law**

An agreement between two or more people to commit a crime, often with an overt act requirement. The offense is complete before the target crime is finished.

*In use:* The text messages formed the agreement needed for conspiracy.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/conspiracy) · related: attempt, solicitation

### `duress`

**Criminal Law**

A defense based on committing a crime because another person threatened imminent serious harm. It is usually unavailable for intentional homicide.

*In use:* The defendant raised duress after the gang threatened to kill him unless he drove.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/duress) · related: necessity, excuse

### `felony`

**Criminal Law**

A serious crime, usually punishable by death or imprisonment for more than one year. Felony status affects grading, sentencing, and collateral consequences.

*In use:* The theft became a felony because the value exceeded the statutory threshold.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/felony) · related: misdemeanor, sentencing

### `general intent`

**Criminal Law**

A mental state requiring awareness of the conduct, but not a further special purpose beyond doing the act. Battery is the classic classroom example.

*In use:* The exam asked whether the offense required general intent or specific intent.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/intent) · related: specific intent, mens rea

### `heat of passion`

**Criminal Law**

A provoked emotional state that can reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter when a reasonable person would lose self-control. The doctrine focuses on adequate provocation and no cooling-off period.

*In use:* The jury considered whether the killing occurred in the heat of passion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/provocation) · related: manslaughter, malice aforethought

### `homicide`

**Criminal Law**

The killing of one human being by another. Criminal law divides homicide into murder, manslaughter, and justified or excused killings.

*In use:* Self-defense made the homicide lawful rather than criminal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/homicide) · related: murder, manslaughter

### `insanity defense`

**Criminal Law**

An excuse defense asserting that mental disease or defect made the defendant legally nonresponsible. Tests vary, but they usually ask about understanding wrongfulness or controlling conduct.

*In use:* The insanity defense turned on whether he knew the act was wrong.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/insanity_defense) · related: mens rea, excuse

### `larceny`

**Criminal Law**

The trespassory taking and carrying away of another's personal property with intent to permanently deprive the owner. Modern theft statutes often merge larceny with embezzlement and false pretenses.

*In use:* Pocketing the watch from the counter was classic larceny.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/larceny) · related: theft, robbery

### `malice aforethought`

**Criminal Law**

The mental state traditionally required for murder. It includes intent to kill, intent to cause serious bodily injury, depraved-heart recklessness, and felony murder.

*In use:* Extreme indifference to human life supplied malice aforethought.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/malice_aforethought) · related: murder, manslaughter

### `manslaughter`

**Criminal Law**

An unlawful killing that is less culpable than murder. Voluntary manslaughter often involves provocation; involuntary manslaughter often involves reckless or negligent killing.

*In use:* Adequate provocation reduced the charge from murder to manslaughter.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/manslaughter) · related: murder, homicide

### `mens rea`

**Criminal Law**

The mental element of a crime, commonly purpose, knowledge, recklessness, or negligence. It pairs with actus reus to separate blameworthy conduct from accident.

*In use:* The word "knowingly" set the mens rea for the possession offense.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mens_rea) · related: actus reus, intent

### `merger`

**Criminal Law**

The absorption of a lesser offense into a greater one when both are based on the same conduct. Merger prevents multiple punishments for the same criminal act.

*In use:* The assault merged into the completed robbery at sentencing.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/merger) · related: lesser included offense, double jeopardy

### `misdemeanor`

**Criminal Law**

A less serious crime, usually punishable by jail time of one year or less, a fine, or both. The line between misdemeanor and felony is set by statute.

*In use:* Simple possession was charged as a misdemeanor under the statute.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/misdemeanor) · related: felony, sentencing

### `mistake of fact`

**Criminal Law**

A factual mistake that may negate the required mens rea. Its effect depends on whether the offense requires purpose, knowledge, recklessness, negligence, or strict liability.

*In use:* Taking the wrong umbrella by mistake of fact defeated intent to steal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mistake_of_fact) · related: mens rea, intent

### `murder`

**Criminal Law**

The unlawful killing of another human being with malice aforethought. Modern statutes usually divide murder into degrees.

*In use:* Premeditation elevated the killing to first-degree murder.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/murder) · related: malice aforethought, homicide

### `necessity`

**Criminal Law**

A justification defense for choosing the lesser evil in an emergency. The defendant argues that violating the law avoided a greater imminent harm.

*In use:* Breaking into the cabin during the blizzard raised a necessity defense.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/necessity) · related: duress, justification

### `omission`

**Criminal Law**

A failure to act that can satisfy actus reus only when the defendant had a legal duty to act. Duties can arise from statute, relationship, contract, voluntary care, or creating the risk.

*In use:* The parent's omission mattered because she had a legal duty to feed the child.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/omission) · related: actus reus, duty

### `premeditation`

**Criminal Law**

Thought or reflection before a killing. In many statutes, premeditation and deliberation distinguish first-degree murder from lesser homicide.

*In use:* Waiting outside with a loaded weapon supported premeditation.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/premeditation) · related: murder, malice aforethought

### `public welfare offense`

**Criminal Law**

A regulatory offense, often involving health, safety, or licensing, that may impose liability with little or no mens rea. Courts treat these as narrow exceptions to the usual mental-state requirement.

*In use:* Selling mislabeled food was treated as a public welfare offense.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/strict_liability) · related: strict liability, mens rea

### `recklessness`

**Criminal Law**

Conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustifiable risk. It is more culpable than negligence because the defendant is aware of the risk and proceeds anyway.

*In use:* Firing into a crowded room showed recklessness even without a purpose to kill.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/recklessness) · related: negligence, mens rea

### `robbery`

**Criminal Law**

The taking of property from a person or presence by force or threat. Robbery is theft plus the added danger of confrontation.

*In use:* Snatching the wallet after pointing a knife made the theft robbery.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/robbery) · related: larceny, burglary

### `self-defense`

**Criminal Law**

A justification for using reasonable force to prevent imminent unlawful force. Deadly force generally requires a reasonable belief of imminent death or serious bodily harm.

*In use:* The defendant claimed self-defense after the victim raised the bat.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/self-defense) · related: necessity, justification

### `solicitation`

**Criminal Law**

Asking, encouraging, or commanding another person to commit a crime with the intent that the crime be committed. The offense is complete when the request is made.

*In use:* Offering money for a killing was solicitation even though the listener refused.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/solicitation) · related: attempt, conspiracy

### `specific intent`

**Criminal Law**

A mental state requiring not only the act, but a further purpose or objective. Burglary's intent to commit a crime inside is the standard example.

*In use:* Voluntary intoxication mattered only if the charge required specific intent.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/intent) · related: general intent, mens rea

### `strict liability`

**Criminal Law**

Liability without proof of a culpable mental state for at least one element of the offense. It is common in minor regulatory crimes but disfavored for serious offenses.

*In use:* The speeding statute imposed strict liability as to the driver's awareness of speed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/strict_liability) · related: mens rea, public welfare offense

### `transferred intent`

**Criminal Law**

A doctrine that transfers intent from the intended target to the actual victim. If the defendant aims at A but hits B, the intent can follow the harm.

*In use:* Transferred intent allowed the murder charge when the bullet killed a bystander.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/transferred_intent) · related: intent, battery
