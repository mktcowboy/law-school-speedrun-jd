# Business Associations Vocabulary

Back to the [doctrine vocabulary index](./README.md) · [vocabulary overview](../README.md).

High-frequency terms for Business Associations: agency, partnerships, corporations, LLCs, fiduciary duties, and owner litigation.

### `actual authority`

**Business Associations**

Authority an agent reasonably believes they have because of the principal's manifestations to the agent. It can be express or implied from the job, past practice, or necessary steps to carry out instructions.

*In use:* The purchasing manager had actual authority to order inventory.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/actual_authority) · related: agency, apparent authority

### `agency`

**Business Associations**

A relationship in which one person, the agent, acts on behalf of and subject to the control of another, the principal. Agency is the backbone of entity liability and authority questions.

*In use:* The salesperson's agency relationship let the company be bound by the contract.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/agency) · related: agent, principal

### `agent`

**Business Associations**

A person authorized to act on behalf of a principal. The agent may bind the principal when acting within actual or apparent authority.

*In use:* The broker acted as the seller's agent in negotiating the deal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/agent) · related: principal, agency

### `apparent authority`

**Business Associations**

Authority a third party reasonably believes an agent has because of the principal's manifestations. The focus is on what the principal communicated to the outside world, not secret limits placed on the agent.

*In use:* The officer had apparent authority because the company held her out as vice president of sales.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/apparent_authority) · related: actual authority, estoppel

### `articles of incorporation`

**Business Associations**

The public charter document filed with the state to create a corporation. It usually states the corporation's name, registered agent, authorized shares, and basic structure.

*In use:* The articles of incorporation authorized ten million shares of common stock.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/articles_of_incorporation) · related: corporation, bylaws

### `board of directors`

**Business Associations**

The elected body that manages or oversees the corporation's business and affairs. Directors set major policy, appoint officers, and owe fiduciary duties to the corporation.

*In use:* The board of directors approved the merger after reviewing the banker presentation.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/board_of_directors) · related: duty of care, business judgment rule

### `business judgment rule`

**Business Associations**

A deferential rule protecting disinterested, informed director decisions made in good faith. It keeps courts from second-guessing ordinary business risk.

*In use:* The failed expansion was protected by the business judgment rule.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/business_judgment_rule) · related: duty of care, fiduciary duty

### `bylaws`

**Business Associations**

Internal rules governing how a corporation operates. Bylaws typically cover meetings, voting procedures, officer roles, and board mechanics.

*In use:* The bylaws required ten days' notice before a special shareholder meeting.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bylaws) · related: articles of incorporation, quorum

### `close corporation`

**Business Associations**

A corporation with a small number of shareholders and no active public trading market for its shares. Close corporations often blur the line between ownership, employment, and management.

*In use:* The freeze-out claim arose in a family-owned close corporation.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/close_corporation) · related: shareholder, fiduciary duty

### `corporation`

**Business Associations**

A separate legal entity created under state law, owned by shareholders and managed under board authority. Its separate personhood usually gives owners limited liability.

*In use:* The corporation owned the factory, not the individual shareholders.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/corporation) · related: shareholder, limited liability

### `derivative action`

**Business Associations**

A lawsuit brought by an owner on behalf of the entity for harm done to the entity. Any recovery generally goes to the entity, not directly to the suing shareholder or member.

*In use:* The shareholder filed a derivative action after the board refused to sue the faithless officer.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/derivative_action) · related: shareholder derivative suit, fiduciary duty

### `dividend`

**Business Associations**

A distribution of corporate earnings or assets to shareholders. Dividends are usually declared by the board and are subject to statutory solvency limits.

*In use:* The board declared a cash dividend after a profitable quarter.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/dividend) · related: shareholder, distribution

### `dissolution`

**Business Associations**

The legal winding up and ending of an entity's existence. Dissolution may be voluntary, judicial, or triggered by statute or the governing agreement.

*In use:* After deadlock, one member petitioned for dissolution of the LLC.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/dissolution) · related: winding up, partnership

### `duty of care`

**Business Associations**

A fiduciary duty requiring managers to act with the care a reasonably prudent person would use in similar circumstances. In corporations, the business judgment rule often shields ordinary care-based claims.

*In use:* The directors satisfied the duty of care by reading the materials and asking questions before voting.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/duty_of_care) · related: business judgment rule, fiduciary duty

### `duty of loyalty`

**Business Associations**

A fiduciary duty requiring managers and agents to put the entity's or principal's interests ahead of their own in conflicted matters. Classic loyalty issues include self-dealing, usurpation of opportunities, and secret profits.

*In use:* Taking the lease opportunity personally breached the officer's duty of loyalty.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/duty_of_loyalty) · related: fiduciary duty, conflict of interest

### `fiduciary duty`

**Business Associations**

A legal duty of trust, loyalty, and care owed by someone who acts for another. Directors, officers, partners, agents, and LLC managers may owe fiduciary duties depending on the relationship and governing law.

*In use:* The managing partner owed a fiduciary duty when allocating firm business.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fiduciary_duty) · related: duty of care, duty of loyalty

### `general partnership`

**Business Associations**

An association of two or more persons carrying on as co-owners of a business for profit. Unless modified by statute or agreement, partners share management and are personally liable for partnership obligations.

*In use:* The handshake venture became a general partnership even without formal papers.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/partnership) · related: partnership, limited partnership

### `incorporation`

**Business Associations**

The process of forming a corporation by filing required documents with the state. Proper incorporation creates the entity's separate legal existence.

*In use:* Incorporation occurred once the secretary of state accepted the filing.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/incorporation) · related: articles of incorporation, corporation

### `limited liability`

**Business Associations**

The rule that owners generally risk only their investment and are not personally liable for entity debts. It is strongest when the entity is respected as separate and formalities are observed where required.

*In use:* Limited liability protected the shareholder from the corporation's unpaid vendor bill.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/limited_liability) · related: corporation, piercing the corporate veil

### `limited liability company`

**Business Associations**

A flexible unincorporated entity that usually combines partnership-style tax and governance flexibility with limited liability for members. Its internal rules often come from an operating agreement.

*In use:* The founders chose a limited liability company so they could customize management rights.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/limited_liability_company_%28llc%29) · related: operating agreement, limited liability

### `limited partnership`

**Business Associations**

A partnership with at least one general partner and at least one limited partner. Limited partners usually get limited liability, while general partners manage and remain personally liable.

*In use:* The real estate fund used a limited partnership with an LLC as general partner.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/limited_partnership) · related: general partnership, limited liability

### `merger`

**Business Associations**

A statutory transaction combining entities so one survives or a new entity results. Merger doctrine often raises approval, appraisal, fiduciary duty, and disclosure issues.

*In use:* Shareholders challenged the merger price as unfair.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/merger) · related: board of directors, appraisal

### `partnership`

**Business Associations**

A business association of co-owners operating for profit. Partnership status can arise from conduct even if the parties never use the word "partnership."

*In use:* Sharing profits and control supported finding a partnership.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/partnership) · related: general partnership, partner

### `piercing the corporate veil`

**Business Associations**

An equitable doctrine allowing a court to disregard entity separateness and hold owners personally liable. Courts usually require misuse of the entity, such as fraud, undercapitalization, or treating entity assets as personal assets.

*In use:* The creditor tried piercing the corporate veil after the owner drained the company account.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/piercing_the_corporate_veil) · related: limited liability, alter ego

### `principal`

**Business Associations**

The person or entity on whose behalf an agent acts. A principal may be bound by authorized acts of the agent and may be liable for some agent conduct.

*In use:* The company was the principal, and the sales representative was its agent.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/principal) · related: agent, agency

### `promoter`

**Business Associations**

A person who helps organize a corporation before it exists, often by arranging contracts, financing, or formation steps. Promoters can face personal liability on pre-incorporation contracts unless properly released.

*In use:* The promoter signed the office lease before the corporation was formed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/promoter) · related: incorporation, ratification

### `proxy`

**Business Associations**

Authority given by a shareholder for another person to vote the shareholder's shares. Public-company proxy rules are a major part of shareholder voting and corporate control.

*In use:* The activist investor solicited proxies to replace two directors.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/proxy) · related: shareholder, voting

### `quorum`

**Business Associations**

The minimum participation required for a meeting to conduct valid business. Quorum rules appear in statutes, bylaws, operating agreements, and partnership agreements.

*In use:* Without a quorum, the board could not approve the transaction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quorum) · related: bylaws, board of directors

### `ratification`

**Business Associations**

Later approval of an act that was done without authority or with a defect in authority. Ratification can bind the principal or entity as if authority existed from the start.

*In use:* The board's later vote ratified the officer's unauthorized contract.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ratification) · related: actual authority, promoter

### `shareholder`

**Business Associations**

An owner of corporate shares. Shareholders usually vote on directors and fundamental changes, but do not manage day-to-day corporate business.

*In use:* As a shareholder, she could vote for directors but could not order the CEO to sign a contract.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/shareholder) · related: corporation, derivative action

### `ultra vires`

**Business Associations** · lit. "beyond the powers"

An act beyond the legal power or authority of a corporation or its agents. Modern law narrows the doctrine, but it still appears in questions about entity powers and unauthorized conduct.

*In use:* The shareholder argued the guaranty was ultra vires under the corporation's charter.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ultra_vires) · related: corporate powers, authority
