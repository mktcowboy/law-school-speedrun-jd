# Criminal Procedure Vocabulary

Back to the [doctrine vocabulary index](./README.md) · [vocabulary overview](../README.md).

High-frequency terms for Criminal Procedure: police investigation, charging, counsel, pleas, and trial protections.

### `arraignment`

**Criminal Procedure**

The early court appearance where the defendant hears the charges and enters a plea. It is also a point where counsel, bail, and scheduling issues may be addressed.

*In use:* At arraignment, the defendant pleaded not guilty and requested appointed counsel.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/arraignment) · related: plea, indictment

### `arrest`

**Criminal Procedure**

A seizure of a person that restrains freedom of movement by legal authority. Arrests usually require probable cause, with or without a warrant depending on the circumstances.

*In use:* The officer made the arrest after matching the suspect to the robbery description.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/arrest) · related: probable cause, seizure

### `bail`

**Criminal Procedure**

Release before trial, often conditioned on money, supervision, or other terms meant to assure appearance and protect the public. Modern bail doctrine also raises due process and equal protection concerns when detention turns on poverty.

*In use:* The judge reduced bail after finding the original amount effectively detained the defendant.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bail) · related: pretrial detention, arraignment

### `custodial interrogation`

**Criminal Procedure**

Questioning by law enforcement after a suspect is in custody or otherwise deprived of freedom in a significant way. Miranda warnings are required before custodial interrogation.

*In use:* The interview became a custodial interrogation once officers blocked the door and accused him of the shooting.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/custodial_interrogation) · related: Miranda warning, right to counsel

### `double jeopardy`

**Criminal Procedure**

The Fifth Amendment protection against being tried or punished twice for the same offense by the same sovereign. The doctrine turns on when jeopardy attaches and whether the later charge is legally the same offense.

*In use:* A second prosecution after acquittal would violate double jeopardy.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/double_jeopardy) · related: acquittal, Fifth Amendment

### `exclusionary rule`

**Criminal Procedure**

The rule that evidence obtained in violation of the Constitution may be kept out of the prosecution's case. It is a deterrence remedy, not an automatic answer to every police mistake.

*In use:* The defense invoked the exclusionary rule after the warrantless search.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/exclusionary_rule) · related: suppression motion, fruit of the poisonous tree

### `exigent circumstances`

**Criminal Procedure**

Emergency conditions that may justify a warrantless search, entry, or arrest when waiting for a warrant would risk harm, escape, or destruction of evidence. The exception is limited by the actual urgency of the situation.

*In use:* Officers relied on exigent circumstances when they heard screaming inside the apartment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/exigent_circumstances) · related: warrant requirement, search and seizure

### `fruit of the poisonous tree`

**Criminal Procedure**

Evidence later discovered because of an earlier constitutional violation. It may be excluded unless the taint is removed by an exception such as attenuation, independent source, or inevitable discovery.

*In use:* The gun was fruit of the poisonous tree because police found it only after the illegal stop.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fruit_of_the_poisonous_tree) · related: exclusionary rule, suppression motion

### `grand jury`

**Criminal Procedure**

A body that investigates and decides whether there is enough evidence to charge a serious crime. In federal felony cases, the Fifth Amendment generally requires indictment by grand jury.

*In use:* The grand jury returned an indictment after hearing from three witnesses.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/grand_jury) · related: indictment, probable cause

### `indictment`

**Criminal Procedure**

A formal criminal charge issued by a grand jury. It states the offense and gives the defendant notice of what the government must prove.

*In use:* The indictment charged conspiracy and wire fraud.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/indictment) · related: grand jury, information

### `information`

**Criminal Procedure**

A formal charging document filed by a prosecutor rather than a grand jury. Many states use informations for some or all criminal charges.

*In use:* The prosecutor filed an information after the defendant waived indictment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/information) · related: indictment, preliminary hearing

### `Miranda warning`

**Criminal Procedure**

The warnings police must give before custodial interrogation: the right to remain silent, that statements can be used against the suspect, and the right to counsel. A valid waiver must be knowing, voluntary, and intelligent.

*In use:* The confession was suppressed because officers questioned him without a Miranda warning.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/miranda_warning) · related: custodial interrogation, self-incrimination

### `plain view doctrine`

**Criminal Procedure**

A warrant exception allowing officers lawfully present in a place to seize evidence whose incriminating character is immediately apparent. The doctrine does not let officers create a view by entering unlawfully.

*In use:* The drugs on the coffee table were seized under the plain view doctrine.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/search_and_seizure) · related: warrant exception, seizure

### `plea bargain`

**Criminal Procedure**

An agreement in which the defendant pleads guilty, often in exchange for reduced charges, a sentencing recommendation, or dismissal of other counts. Plea bargaining resolves most criminal cases.

*In use:* The plea bargain dismissed the felony count in exchange for a misdemeanor plea.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/plea_bargain) · related: arraignment, guilty plea

### `preliminary hearing`

**Criminal Procedure**

A hearing where a judge decides whether there is enough evidence to proceed on the charge. It is a screening device, not a full trial on guilt.

*In use:* At the preliminary hearing, the court found probable cause to bind the case over for trial.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/preliminary_hearing) · related: probable cause, information

### `probable cause`

**Criminal Procedure**

A fair probability, based on facts, that a crime occurred or that evidence will be found in a place. Probable cause is required for arrests and search warrants.

*In use:* The affidavit established probable cause to search the storage unit.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/probable_cause) · related: reasonable suspicion, search warrant

### `reasonable suspicion`

**Criminal Procedure**

A particularized and objective basis for suspecting criminal activity. It is less demanding than probable cause but requires more than a hunch.

*In use:* Nervousness alone did not create reasonable suspicion for the stop.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/reasonable_suspicion) · related: stop and frisk, probable cause

### `right to counsel`

**Criminal Procedure**

The constitutional right to legal assistance in criminal proceedings. It appears in different forms under the Fifth Amendment during custodial interrogation and under the Sixth Amendment after formal charges.

*In use:* Once the right to counsel attached, police could not deliberately elicit statements without counsel present.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/right_to_counsel) · related: Miranda warning, Sixth Amendment

### `search and seizure`

**Criminal Procedure**

Fourth Amendment doctrine governing government intrusions on privacy, property, and liberty. It asks whether a search or seizure occurred and whether it was reasonable.

*In use:* The phone search raised a search and seizure issue under the Fourth Amendment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/search_and_seizure) · related: warrant requirement, probable cause

### `search incident to arrest`

**Criminal Procedure**

A warrant exception allowing officers to search an arrestee and nearby areas for weapons, evidence, or means of escape. The scope depends on the place and the reasons for the search.

*In use:* The backpack search was defended as a search incident to arrest.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/search_and_seizure) · related: arrest, warrant exception

### `search warrant`

**Criminal Procedure**

A judge's authorization to search a particular place for particular items based on probable cause. Warrants must be supported by oath or affirmation and describe the place and things with particularity.

*In use:* The search warrant listed the apartment and the stolen laptops.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/search_warrant) · related: probable cause, warrant requirement

### `seizure`

**Criminal Procedure**

A meaningful interference with a person's liberty or possessory interest in property. Stops, arrests, and taking physical evidence can all be seizures.

*In use:* The traffic stop was a seizure even though it lasted only eight minutes.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/search_and_seizure) · related: arrest, stop and frisk

### `speedy trial`

**Criminal Procedure**

The Sixth Amendment right to be tried without unreasonable delay after accusation. Courts weigh delay length, reasons, assertion of the right, and prejudice.

*In use:* A five-year delay forced the court to analyze the speedy trial factors.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/speedy_trial) · related: Sixth Amendment, dismissal

### `stop and frisk`

**Criminal Procedure**

A brief investigative stop based on reasonable suspicion, paired with a limited pat-down for weapons if officers reasonably suspect the person is armed and dangerous. It comes from Terry v. Ohio.

*In use:* The officer had reasonable suspicion for a stop and frisk after seeing the suspect casing parked cars.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/stop_and_frisk) · related: reasonable suspicion, seizure

### `suppression motion`

**Criminal Procedure**

A defense request to keep evidence out of trial, usually because it was obtained illegally or in violation of constitutional rules. Suppression litigation often decides the practical strength of the prosecution's case.

*In use:* The suppression motion challenged the confession and the gun found afterward.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/exclusionary_rule) · related: exclusionary rule, fruit of the poisonous tree

### `warrant requirement`

**Criminal Procedure**

The general Fourth Amendment preference that searches and seizures be authorized in advance by a neutral magistrate. Many exam issues ask whether an exception justified acting without a warrant.

*In use:* The home entry violated the warrant requirement unless exigent circumstances applied.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fourth_amendment) · related: search warrant, exigent circumstances
