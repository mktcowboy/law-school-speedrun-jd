# Professional Responsibility Vocabulary

Back to the [doctrine vocabulary](./README.md) · [vocabulary overview](../README.md).

Terms that recur in legal ethics, lawyer regulation, conflicts, fees, confidentiality, and discipline.

### `ABA Model Rules`

**Professional Responsibility**

The ABA Model Rules are influential model ethics rules that many states use as the template for binding lawyer regulation. They are not law by themselves unless a jurisdiction adopts them.

*In use:* A professional-responsibility exam often asks for the Model Rule approach and then notes that state variations may matter.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: legal ethics, disciplinary authority, professional misconduct

### `attorney-client privilege`

**Professional Responsibility**

Attorney-client privilege protects confidential lawyer-client communications made for the purpose of seeking or giving legal advice. The client generally controls whether to invoke or waive the privilege.

*In use:* A lawyer may refuse to produce privileged emails in discovery unless the client waives the protection.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/attorney-client_privilege) · related: confidentiality, waiver, work product doctrine

### `candor toward the tribunal`

**Professional Responsibility**

Candor toward the tribunal is the lawyer's duty not to lie to a court, offer false evidence, or hide controlling legal authority when disclosure is required. It can override ordinary client-centered advocacy.

*In use:* A lawyer cannot knowingly let a client testify falsely and simply call it zealous advocacy.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: false evidence, tribunal, professional misconduct

### `client confidentiality`

**Professional Responsibility**

Client confidentiality is the ethics duty to protect information relating to the representation, even when that information is not privileged. It is broader than attorney-client privilege and usually continues after the representation ends.

*In use:* A lawyer should not discuss a client's matter in an elevator even if no litigation privilege issue is present.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: attorney-client privilege, informed consent, former client

### `client trust account`

**Professional Responsibility**

A client trust account is a separate account used to hold client or third-party funds that do not belong to the lawyer. Keeping those funds separate prevents commingling and misuse.

*In use:* Settlement proceeds must go into the lawyer's trust account until fees and client shares are properly distributed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: safekeeping property, commingling, IOLTA

### `competence`

**Professional Responsibility**

Competence is the duty to provide representation with the legal knowledge, skill, thoroughness, and preparation reasonably necessary for the matter. A lawyer may become competent through study, association, or reasonable preparation.

*In use:* A lawyer taking a new kind of case must either learn the field, associate with competent counsel, or decline the work.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: diligence, malpractice, limited-scope representation

### `concurrent conflict of interest`

**Professional Responsibility**

A concurrent conflict exists when representing one client is directly adverse to another current client or materially limited by another duty or interest. Some conflicts are consentable after disclosure; others are not.

*In use:* A firm usually cannot represent both sides of the same lawsuit, even if both clients ask it to.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/conflict_of_interest) · related: informed consent, loyalty, imputed conflict

### `conflict of interest`

**Professional Responsibility**

A conflict of interest arises when a lawyer's duties to one client, another client, a former client, a third person, or the lawyer's own interests pull against the representation. The central question is whether the conflict is prohibited, consentable, or waivable.

*In use:* A lawyer with a personal financial stake in the deal must analyze conflict rules before advising the client.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/conflict_of_interest) · related: concurrent conflict, former-client conflict, informed consent

### `contingency fee`

**Professional Responsibility**

A contingency fee is a fee that depends on the outcome, usually a percentage of the recovery. Ethics rules regulate the writing, reasonableness, and subject areas where contingency fees are forbidden.

*In use:* A personal-injury lawyer may charge a contingency fee, but a criminal-defense lawyer cannot make the fee contingent on acquittal.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/contingency_fee) · related: fee agreement, reasonableness, retainer

### `diligence`

**Professional Responsibility**

Diligence is the lawyer's duty to pursue a client's matter with reasonable promptness and attention. Missing deadlines, ignoring clients, or letting a matter stagnate can violate the duty even without bad intent.

*In use:* A lawyer who repeatedly fails to respond to court deadlines risks discipline for lack of diligence.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: competence, communication, malpractice

### `fiduciary duty`

**Professional Responsibility**

A fiduciary duty is a duty of loyalty, care, honesty, and good faith owed by someone trusted to act for another. Lawyers owe fiduciary duties to clients because they control legal rights, confidential information, and client property.

*In use:* Secretly profiting from a client's transaction can breach the lawyer's fiduciary duty.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fiduciary_duty) · related: loyalty, conflict of interest, client trust account

### `former-client conflict`

**Professional Responsibility**

A former-client conflict limits a lawyer from representing a new client against a former client in the same or a substantially related matter. The rule protects loyalty and confidential information after the representation ends.

*In use:* A lawyer who drafted a contract for a company may be barred from later attacking that same contract for an opposing party.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/conflict_of_interest) · related: confidentiality, informed consent, screening

### `imputed conflict`

**Professional Responsibility**

An imputed conflict is a conflict attributed from one lawyer to other lawyers in the same firm. The rule treats a firm as sharing certain loyalties and confidential information unless an exception or screen applies.

*In use:* If one lawyer is conflicted from representing the plaintiff, the entire firm may be conflicted too.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/conflict_of_interest) · related: screening, firm, former-client conflict

### `informed consent`

**Professional Responsibility**

Informed consent is a client's agreement after the lawyer communicates enough information about risks and alternatives for a reasonable decision. Many conflict waivers and confidentiality disclosures require it, often confirmed in writing.

*In use:* A client cannot waive a conflict through informed consent unless the lawyer explains how the conflict could affect the representation.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/informed_consent) · related: conflict of interest, waiver, communication

### `IOLTA`

**Professional Responsibility**

IOLTA means Interest on Lawyers' Trust Accounts, a system for pooling small or short-term client funds so interest supports legal-aid programs. The funds remain client property even though the interest is handled by rule.

*In use:* A small retainer held briefly may be placed in an IOLTA account rather than a separate interest-bearing account.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: client trust account, safekeeping property, commingling

### `legal malpractice`

**Professional Responsibility**

Legal malpractice is professional negligence or misconduct by a lawyer that causes harm to a client. It is a civil liability concept, distinct from bar discipline, though the same facts may support both.

*In use:* Missing the statute of limitations may create a legal-malpractice claim if the client loses a viable case.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_malpractice) · related: competence, diligence, fiduciary duty

### `limited-scope representation`

**Professional Responsibility**

Limited-scope representation lets a lawyer handle only part of a client's matter if the limitation is reasonable and the client gives informed consent. It can make legal help more affordable, but the boundaries must be clear.

*In use:* A lawyer may agree to draft a motion without appearing for the whole case if local rules and ethics rules allow it.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: informed consent, competence, communication

### `loyalty`

**Professional Responsibility**

Loyalty is the lawyer's duty to put the client's lawful interests ahead of competing duties and personal interests within the representation. Conflict rules are the main way professional responsibility enforces loyalty.

*In use:* A lawyer violates loyalty by secretly representing a competitor in a matter directly adverse to the client.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fiduciary_duty) · related: conflict of interest, fiduciary duty, concurrent conflict

### `mandatory withdrawal`

**Professional Responsibility**

Mandatory withdrawal is required when continuing the representation would violate law or ethics rules, the lawyer is unable to represent the client, or the lawyer is discharged. Court permission may still be needed in litigation.

*In use:* A lawyer must withdraw if the client insists on using the lawyer's services to commit fraud.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: permissive withdrawal, tribunal, client fraud

### `pro bono`

**Professional Responsibility** · lit. "for the public good"

Pro bono work is legal service provided without charge or at a reduced charge, usually for people or causes lacking access to counsel. Ethics rules encourage pro bono service, though the exact obligation is usually aspirational.

*In use:* A lawyer may count representation of an indigent tenant as pro bono service.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pro_bono) · related: access to justice, legal aid, professionalism

### `retainer`

**Professional Responsibility**

A retainer is money paid to secure or pay for legal services, though the ethics treatment depends on whether it is earned on receipt, an advance fee, or security for future fees. Mislabeling a retainer can create trust-account problems.

*In use:* An advance fee retainer often must stay in trust until the lawyer earns it.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/retainer) · related: fee agreement, client trust account, contingency fee

### `safekeeping property`

**Professional Responsibility**

Safekeeping property is the lawyer's duty to identify, segregate, preserve, and account for client or third-party property. The duty covers money in trust as well as documents or other items held for a client.

*In use:* A lawyer must promptly notify the client and keep disputed settlement funds separate until the dispute is resolved.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: client trust account, commingling, IOLTA

### `screening`

**Professional Responsibility**

Screening is the isolation of a conflicted lawyer from a matter so that confidential information is not shared with the rest of the firm. Whether screening cures a conflict depends on the type of conflict and the jurisdiction's rules.

*In use:* A firm hiring a lateral lawyer may screen that lawyer from a matter involving the lawyer's former client.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/conflict_of_interest) · related: imputed conflict, former-client conflict, confidentiality

### `solicitation`

**Professional Responsibility**

Solicitation is targeted outreach by a lawyer to seek professional employment, especially when directed to someone who may need legal services. Ethics rules restrict coercive, misleading, or certain real-time solicitations.

*In use:* A lawyer chasing accident victims in person for paid representation raises a solicitation problem.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: advertising, professional misconduct, undue influence

### `unauthorized practice of law`

**Professional Responsibility**

Unauthorized practice of law is practicing law without the required license or helping someone else do so. The doctrine protects clients and courts, but it also raises hard questions about multijurisdictional practice and legal technology.

*In use:* A lawyer licensed only in one state must check local rules before opening a regular practice in another.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/legal_ethics) · related: admission to practice, multijurisdictional practice, competence

### `work product doctrine`

**Professional Responsibility**

The work product doctrine protects materials prepared by or for a lawyer in anticipation of litigation. It is related to, but distinct from, attorney-client privilege because it protects litigation preparation rather than only confidential legal advice communications.

*In use:* Opposing counsel may not be able to obtain an attorney's interview notes prepared for trial.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/attorney-client_privilege) · related: attorney-client privilege, discovery, litigation strategy
