# Property Vocabulary

Back to the [doctrine vocabulary index](./README.md).

High-frequency property terms for reading cases, building outlines, and spotting exam issues.

### `adverse possession`

**Property**

Adverse possession allows a possessor to acquire title to land by possessing it for the statutory period in a way that is actual, open, notorious, exclusive, continuous, and hostile. The details vary by jurisdiction.

*In use:* The fence-line dispute turned on whether the neighbor's twenty years of use satisfied adverse possession.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/adverse_possession) · related: color of title, title, possession

### `bailment`

**Property**

Bailment is temporary transfer of possession of personal property without transfer of ownership. The bailee must usually use the level of care the law requires for the kind of bailment.

*In use:* Leaving a car with a valet creates a bailment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bailment) · related: personal property, possession, conversion

### `color of title`

**Property**

Color of title is an apparent but defective claim to title, usually based on a flawed deed or other document. It can matter in adverse possession statutes.

*In use:* The possessor relied on color of title because the deed described the wrong boundary.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/color_of_title) · related: adverse possession, deed, title

### `concurrent estate`

**Property**

A concurrent estate is ownership or possession of the same property by more than one person at the same time. Tenancy in common, joint tenancy, and tenancy by the entirety are the main forms.

*In use:* The siblings held a concurrent estate after inheriting the house together.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/concurrent_estate) · related: joint tenancy, tenancy in common, tenancy by the entirety

### `covenant`

**Property**

A covenant is a promise connected to land, often restricting or requiring certain uses. Some covenants can run with the land and bind later owners.

*In use:* The subdivision covenant barred commercial use of the lots.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/covenant) · related: restrictive covenant, easement, servitude

### `deed`

**Property**

A deed is a written instrument used to transfer ownership of real property. Valid deeds usually require identification of the parties and land, words of conveyance, signature, and delivery.

*In use:* The buyer recorded the deed after closing.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/deed) · related: grantor, grantee, recording acts

### `easement`

**Property**

An easement is a nonpossessory right to use another person's land or restrict its use. Easements can be express, implied, by necessity, or by prescription.

*In use:* The driveway easement allowed the back parcel to reach the road.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/easement) · related: servient estate, dominant estate, license

### `eminent domain`

**Property**

Eminent domain is the government's power to take private property for public use with just compensation. The constitutional questions often involve public use and valuation.

*In use:* The city used eminent domain to acquire land for the highway expansion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/eminent_domain) · related: takings, just compensation, public use

### `estate`

**Property**

An estate is a legally recognized interest in land measured by possession, duration, or future rights. Property courses use the term for present and future ownership interests.

*In use:* A life estate gives present possession only for the life of the measuring person.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/estate) · related: fee simple, life estate, future interest

### `fee simple`

**Property**

Fee simple is the broadest common ownership estate in land. A fee simple absolute can last forever and is freely transferable.

*In use:* "To A and her heirs" traditionally created a fee simple.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fee_simple) · related: estate, life estate, future interest

### `fee tail`

**Property**

Fee tail is an old estate designed to keep land within a family line. Most American jurisdictions have abolished it or convert it into fee simple.

*In use:* The phrase "to A and the heirs of his body" signals a fee tail in classic estates problems.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fee_tail) · related: fee simple, estate, future interest

### `fixture`

**Property**

A fixture is personal property that has become so attached to land or a building that the law treats it as real property. The classification matters in sales, leases, and secured transactions.

*In use:* The built-in furnace was treated as a fixture that passed with the house.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fixture) · related: real property, personal property, accession

### `future interest`

**Property**

A future interest is a property interest that gives possession or enjoyment later, not now. Remainders, reversions, and executory interests are common examples.

*In use:* The deed gave B a future interest after A's life estate ended.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/future_interest) · related: remainder, life estate, rule against perpetuities

### `grantee`

**Property**

A grantee is the person who receives an interest in property through a deed or other conveyance. The grantee is the transferee.

*In use:* The deed named the daughter as grantee.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/grantee) · related: grantor, deed, conveyance

### `grantor`

**Property**

A grantor is the person who transfers an interest in property through a deed or other conveyance. The grantor is the transferor.

*In use:* The grantor signed and delivered the deed at closing.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/grantor) · related: grantee, deed, conveyance

### `joint tenancy`

**Property**

Joint tenancy is a concurrent estate with a right of survivorship. When one joint tenant dies, that tenant's share passes to the surviving joint tenant or tenants rather than through probate.

*In use:* The deed created joint tenancy by naming the owners "as joint tenants with right of survivorship."

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/joint_tenancy) · related: tenancy in common, tenancy by the entirety, concurrent estate

### `leasehold estate`

**Property**

A leasehold estate is a tenant's right to possess property for a limited time under a lease. It separates possession from ownership.

*In use:* The one-year apartment lease gave the tenant a leasehold estate.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/leasehold) · related: landlord, tenant, quiet enjoyment

### `license`

**Property**

A license is permission to enter or use land without creating an estate or easement. It is usually revocable unless another doctrine limits revocation.

*In use:* A concert ticket gives the holder a license to enter the venue.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/license) · related: easement, trespass, consent

### `life estate`

**Property**

A life estate gives possession and use of property for the life of a person. When that life ends, possession shifts to a remainderman or back to the grantor through a reversion.

*In use:* "To A for life, then to B" gives A a life estate and B a remainder.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/life_estate) · related: remainder, reversion, waste

### `mortgage`

**Property**

A mortgage is a security interest in real property used to secure repayment of a debt. If the borrower defaults, the lender may seek foreclosure.

*In use:* The buyer gave the bank a mortgage to finance the purchase price.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mortgage) · related: foreclosure, lien, title

### `partition`

**Property**

Partition is a legal process that divides property among co-owners or orders sale and division of proceeds. It is a common exit right for tenants in common.

*In use:* When the co-owners could not agree on sale, one filed for partition.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/partition) · related: tenancy in common, concurrent estate, joint tenancy

### `possession`

**Property**

Possession is physical control over property plus intent to control it. Property law often separates possession from title.

*In use:* The finder had possession of the watch even though true title remained with the owner.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/possession) · related: title, adverse possession, bailment

### `present possessory estate`

**Property**

A present possessory estate gives the holder the current right to possess land. Fee simple, life estate, and leasehold interests are examples.

*In use:* A held the present possessory estate, while B waited on a future interest.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/estate) · related: fee simple, life estate, future interest

### `quiet enjoyment`

**Property**

Quiet enjoyment is the tenant's right to possess and use leased property without serious interference by the landlord or someone with superior title. It often appears as an implied covenant in leases.

*In use:* Repeated lockouts by the landlord breached the covenant of quiet enjoyment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quiet_enjoyment) · related: leasehold estate, constructive eviction, landlord-tenant

### `recording acts`

**Property**

Recording acts determine priority among competing claims to real property when deeds or other interests are recorded. The major types are race, notice, and race-notice statutes.

*In use:* The second buyer won under the recording act because she bought without notice and recorded first.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/recording_acts) · related: deed, notice, bona fide purchaser

### `remainder`

**Property**

A remainder is a future interest in someone other than the grantor that becomes possessory naturally when a prior estate ends. It commonly follows a life estate.

*In use:* In "to A for life, then to B," B has a remainder.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/remainder) · related: life estate, future interest, reversion

### `rent`

**Property**

Rent is consideration paid by a tenant for possession or use of property. In landlord-tenant law, rent duties often interact with habitability, eviction, and lease covenants.

*In use:* The tenant withheld rent after the landlord failed to repair the heat.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/rent) · related: leasehold estate, quiet enjoyment, landlord-tenant

### `restrictive covenant`

**Property**

A restrictive covenant limits how land may be used. In neighborhoods, restrictive covenants often regulate building size, business use, or aesthetic rules.

*In use:* The restrictive covenant prohibited short-term rentals in the subdivision.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/restrictive_covenant) · related: covenant, servitude, zoning

### `rule against perpetuities`

**Property**

The rule against perpetuities limits future interests that might vest too remotely. The classic formulation asks whether the interest must vest, if at all, within lives in being plus twenty-one years.

*In use:* The gift to A's first grandchild to become a lawyer raised a rule-against-perpetuities issue.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/rule_against_perpetuities) · related: future interest, remainder, vesting

### `tenancy at sufferance`

**Property**

Tenancy at sufferance describes a holdover tenant who originally entered lawfully but stays after the right to possess ends. The landlord may usually evict or treat the holdover as a new tenant under local law.

*In use:* Staying after the lease expired made the renter a tenant at sufferance.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/tenancy_at_sufferance) · related: leasehold estate, holdover tenant, eviction

### `tenancy at will`

**Property**

Tenancy at will is a leasehold that continues only so long as both landlord and tenant wish it to continue. Either side can terminate with proper notice.

*In use:* The informal month-to-month arrangement was treated as a tenancy at will.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/tenancy_at_will) · related: leasehold estate, periodic tenancy, notice

### `tenancy by the entirety`

**Property**

Tenancy by the entirety is a form of marital co-ownership with survivorship rights. Many jurisdictions protect it from unilateral transfer by one spouse.

*In use:* The spouses took title as tenants by the entirety.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/tenancy_by_the_entirety) · related: joint tenancy, tenancy in common, concurrent estate

### `tenancy in common`

**Property**

Tenancy in common is co-ownership where each tenant owns a separate fractional share and has a right to possess the whole. There is no right of survivorship.

*In use:* The friends bought the cabin as tenants in common, each owning a one-half share.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/tenancy_in_common) · related: joint tenancy, partition, concurrent estate

### `title`

**Property**

Title is legal ownership of property or evidence of that ownership. A person can have possession without title, and title can be disputed even when possession is clear.

*In use:* The quiet-title action asked the court to decide who held title to the parcel.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/title) · related: deed, possession, color of title

### `warranty deed`

**Property**

A warranty deed conveys property with promises about the quality of title. The exact warranties depend on whether the deed is general or special.

*In use:* The buyer preferred a warranty deed because it included promises against title defects.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/warranty_deed) · related: deed, title, covenant

### `waste`

**Property**

Waste is conduct by a present possessor that harms the value of property for someone with a future interest. It can be voluntary, permissive, or ameliorative.

*In use:* The life tenant's failure to repair the roof raised a permissive-waste claim.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/waste) · related: life estate, future interest, remainderman

### `zoning`

**Property**

Zoning is local regulation of land use by districts, such as residential, commercial, or industrial zones. Property courses use zoning to study police power, nonconforming uses, variances, and takings pressure points.

*In use:* The owner sought a zoning variance to build closer to the lot line.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/zoning) · related: restrictive covenant, land use, variance
