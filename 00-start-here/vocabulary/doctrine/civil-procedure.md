# Civil Procedure Vocabulary

Back to the [doctrine vocabulary index](./README.md) · [vocabulary overview](../README.md).

Core terms for the 1L civil procedure course: jurisdiction, pleadings, discovery, joinder, motions, judgments, and preclusion.

### `answer`

**Civil Procedure**

The defendant's formal response to the complaint. It admits or denies allegations and raises defenses, counterclaims, and sometimes crossclaims.

*In use:* The answer denied negligence and pleaded the statute of limitations.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/answer) · related: complaint, pleading

### `claim preclusion`

**Civil Procedure**

The rule that a final judgment bars the same parties from relitigating the same claim. It is often called res judicata.

*In use:* Claim preclusion barred the second suit arising from the same accident.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_judicata) · related: collateral estoppel, final judgment

### `class action`

**Civil Procedure**

A lawsuit in which named representatives litigate on behalf of a larger group with common issues. Federal class actions must satisfy Rule 23's requirements.

*In use:* The small individual damages made a class action practical.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/class_action) · related: joinder, adequacy

### `collateral estoppel`

**Civil Procedure**

Issue preclusion: once an issue has been actually litigated and necessarily decided, it cannot be relitigated in a later case by a bound party.

*In use:* Collateral estoppel foreclosed a second fight over who ran the red light.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/collateral_estoppel) · related: claim preclusion, res judicata

### `complaint`

**Civil Procedure**

The plaintiff's opening pleading, which starts a civil lawsuit. It states the claim, the basis for relief, and usually the remedy requested.

*In use:* The complaint alleged breach of contract and demanded damages.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/complaint) · related: answer, pleading

### `counterclaim`

**Civil Procedure**

A claim brought by a defendant against an opposing party, usually the plaintiff. Some counterclaims are compulsory because they arise from the same transaction or occurrence.

*In use:* The defendant answered and filed a counterclaim for unpaid invoices.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/counterclaim) · related: crossclaim, joinder

### `default judgment`

**Civil Procedure**

A judgment entered when a party, usually the defendant, fails to plead or otherwise defend. It is not a merits win after trial; it follows nonappearance or default.

*In use:* The court entered default judgment after the defendant ignored service.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/default_judgment) · related: service of process, answer

### `deposition`

**Civil Procedure**

Out-of-court sworn testimony taken before trial and recorded. Depositions are discovery tools and can also preserve testimony for later use.

*In use:* The witness contradicted her deposition at trial.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/deposition) · related: discovery, interrogatories

### `discovery`

**Civil Procedure**

The pretrial process for obtaining information and evidence from parties and nonparties. It includes depositions, interrogatories, document requests, admissions, and subpoenas.

*In use:* The decisive emails surfaced during discovery.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/discovery) · related: deposition, interrogatories

### `diversity jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

Federal subject-matter jurisdiction based on parties being citizens of different states and the amount in controversy exceeding the statutory threshold. Complete diversity is usually required.

*In use:* Diversity jurisdiction existed because no plaintiff shared citizenship with any defendant.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/diversity_jurisdiction) · related: subject-matter jurisdiction, removal

### `Erie doctrine`

**Civil Procedure**

The rule that a federal court sitting in diversity applies state substantive law and federal procedural law. The hard cases ask whether a rule is substantive, procedural, or outcome-affecting.

*In use:* The statute of limitations question triggered an Erie doctrine analysis.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/erie_doctrine) · related: diversity jurisdiction, federal rules

### `federal question jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

Federal subject-matter jurisdiction over civil actions arising under the Constitution, federal statutes, or treaties. The federal issue must usually appear on the face of a well-pleaded complaint.

*In use:* The patent claim supplied federal question jurisdiction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/federal_question_jurisdiction) · related: subject-matter jurisdiction, well-pleaded complaint

### `forum non conveniens`

**Civil Procedure**

A doctrine allowing dismissal when another forum is substantially more appropriate, even though the current court has jurisdiction and venue. It usually points to a foreign or state forum.

*In use:* The court dismissed on forum non conveniens grounds because the witnesses and accident were abroad.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/forum_non_conveniens) · related: venue, transfer

### `impleader`

**Civil Procedure**

A device allowing a defending party to bring in a third party who may be liable for all or part of the claim against the defender. In federal practice, it is Rule 14.

*In use:* The contractor used impleader to bring the subcontractor into the case.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/impleader) · related: joinder, third-party defendant

### `interpleader`

**Civil Procedure**

A procedure that lets a stakeholder force competing claimants to litigate among themselves over the same property or fund. It protects the stakeholder from multiple liability.

*In use:* The insurer filed interpleader because three people claimed the policy proceeds.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/interpleader) · related: joinder, stakeholder

### `interrogatories`

**Civil Procedure**

Written questions served during discovery that must be answered in writing under oath. They are usually served on parties, not nonparty witnesses.

*In use:* The interrogatories asked the company to identify everyone involved in the sale.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/interrogatories) · related: discovery, deposition

### `joinder`

**Civil Procedure**

The joining of claims or parties in one lawsuit. Joinder rules ask whether the case can and should proceed with everyone and everything together.

*In use:* Joinder was proper because the claims arose from the same collision.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/joinder) · related: impleader, class action

### `long-arm statute`

**Civil Procedure**

A state statute authorizing courts to exercise personal jurisdiction over certain out-of-state defendants. The statute still must operate within due process limits.

*In use:* The long-arm statute reached the company because it shipped products into the state.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/long-arm_statute) · related: personal jurisdiction, minimum contacts

### `minimum contacts`

**Civil Procedure**

The due process baseline for personal jurisdiction over an out-of-state defendant. The defendant must have enough forum contacts that suit there is fair and foreseeable.

*In use:* A single unrelated website view did not create minimum contacts.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/minimum_contacts) · related: personal jurisdiction, long-arm statute

### `motion to dismiss`

**Civil Procedure**

A request to end claims at an early stage, often because the court lacks power or the complaint fails to state a claim. Rule 12 supplies the common federal grounds.

*In use:* The defendant filed a motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/motion_to_dismiss) · related: pleading, complaint

### `personal jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's power to bind a particular defendant. For nonresidents, the modern analysis focuses on minimum contacts, purposeful availment, and fairness.

*In use:* Personal jurisdiction failed because the defendant had no forum contacts.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/personal_jurisdiction) · related: minimum contacts, subject-matter jurisdiction

### `pleading`

**Civil Procedure**

The formal documents that frame the parties' claims and defenses. In federal court, the complaint must give a short and plain statement showing entitlement to relief.

*In use:* The pleading gave notice of the fraud theory but lacked particular facts.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pleading) · related: complaint, answer

### `remand`

**Civil Procedure**

To send a case back to the court or tribunal from which it came. In removal practice, remand returns a case from federal court to state court.

*In use:* The federal court ordered remand because diversity was incomplete.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/remand) · related: removal, subject-matter jurisdiction

### `removal`

**Civil Procedure**

The process by which a defendant moves a case from state court to federal court when federal jurisdiction exists. Removal is statutory and timing-sensitive.

*In use:* The defendant filed removal papers after the complaint revealed a federal claim.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/removal) · related: remand, diversity jurisdiction

### `res judicata`

**Civil Procedure**

The preclusion doctrine that prevents relitigation after a final judgment. In many classrooms, the term is used broadly; more precisely, it often means claim preclusion.

*In use:* Res judicata required dismissal of the second contract suit.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_judicata) · related: claim preclusion, collateral estoppel

### `Rule 11`

**Civil Procedure**

The federal rule requiring lawyers and parties to certify that filings have a proper legal and factual basis and are not filed for an improper purpose. Violations can lead to sanctions.

*In use:* The baseless fraud complaint drew a Rule 11 motion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/rule_11) · related: sanctions, pleading

### `service of process`

**Civil Procedure**

Formal delivery of the summons and complaint to give notice and bring a defendant into the case. Proper service is tied to due process and personal jurisdiction.

*In use:* The default judgment was vacated because service of process was defective.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/service_of_process) · related: summons, personal jurisdiction

### `subject-matter jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's power to hear the type of case before it. Unlike personal jurisdiction, it cannot be waived and may be raised at any time.

*In use:* The court dismissed once it found no subject-matter jurisdiction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/subject_matter_jurisdiction) · related: diversity jurisdiction, federal question jurisdiction

### `summary judgment`

**Civil Procedure**

Judgment without trial when there is no genuine dispute of material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. It tests the proof, not just the pleadings.

*In use:* The video evidence made summary judgment possible.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/summary_judgment) · related: motion, material fact

### `supplemental jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

Federal jurisdiction over additional claims that are part of the same case or controversy as claims already within federal jurisdiction. It keeps related state-law claims in the same federal case.

*In use:* Supplemental jurisdiction covered the state negligence claim tied to the federal claim.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/supplemental_jurisdiction) · related: federal question jurisdiction, diversity jurisdiction

### `venue`

**Civil Procedure**

The proper geographic location for a lawsuit within a court system. Venue is distinct from jurisdiction, which concerns the court's power.

*In use:* Venue was proper in the district where the contract was performed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/venue) · related: forum non conveniens, transfer

### `voir dire`

**Civil Procedure**

Pretrial questioning of potential jurors to uncover bias and select a jury. The term is also used for questioning a witness about qualifications.

*In use:* During voir dire, the judge excused a juror who knew the plaintiff.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/voir_dire) · related: jury selection, challenge for cause
