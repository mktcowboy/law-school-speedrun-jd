The working glossary for the curriculum — the Latin, the jargon, and the terms of art that show up in casebooks, opinions, and exams. Use it as a lookup while you read, not as a list to memorize cold. Each entry links to Cornell LII [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex) for a fuller treatment and citations.

## Latin And Foreign Phrases

### `actus reus`

**Criminal Law** · lit. "guilty act"

The physical element of a crime — a voluntary act or an unlawful failure to act. Pairs with `mens rea`, the mental element; both are generally required, because the law does not punish thought alone.

*In use:* The state proved intent but still had to establish an actus reus.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/actus_reus) · related: mens rea, omission

### `amicus curiae`

**Appeals** · lit. "friend of the court"

A non-party who, with the court's permission, files a brief to supply information or argument on an issue in the case. Common in appellate and Supreme Court litigation.

*In use:* Two dozen states filed amicus curiae briefs on the statute.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/amicus_curiae) · related: brief, standing

### `bona fide`

**General** · lit. "in good faith"

Genuine, honest, and without fraud or notice of a defect. A *bona fide purchaser* buys for value without notice of a competing claim and takes the property free of it.

*In use:* As a bona fide purchaser, she took clear of the earlier lien.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bona_fide_purchaser) · related: good faith, notice

### `caveat emptor`

**Property / Contracts** · lit. "let the buyer beware"

The common-law rule that a buyer bears the risk of a purchase's quality absent fraud or warranty. Heavily narrowed today by warranty law and consumer protection.

*In use:* Caveat emptor once barred the claim, but the implied warranty of habitability changed that.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/caveat_emptor) · related: warranty, as-is sale

### `certiorari`

**Appeals** · lit. "to be more fully informed"

A discretionary writ by which a higher court — most often the U.S. Supreme Court — agrees to review a lower court's decision. Shorthand: "cert granted" or "cert denied."

*In use:* The Court granted certiorari to resolve a split among the circuits.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/certiorari) · related: writ, circuit split

### `de novo`

**Appeals / Civil Procedure** · lit. "anew"

A standard of review under which the appellate court decides the question fresh, with no deference to the court below. Applied to questions of law.

*In use:* Because it was a pure question of law, review was de novo.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/de_novo) · related: standard of review, clear error

### `dictum` (obiter dictum)

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "a remark said in passing"

A statement in an opinion that is not necessary to the result. It is persuasive but not binding, unlike the `holding`. Plural: *dicta*.

*In use:* That line was dictum, so later courts were free to depart from it.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/dictum) · related: holding, ratio decidendi

### `en banc`

**Appeals** · French "on the bench"

A sitting in which all (or most) judges of an appellate court hear a case, instead of the usual three-judge panel. Reserved for questions of exceptional importance.

*In use:* The full circuit reheard the case en banc and vacated the panel decision.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/en_banc) · related: panel, rehearing

### `ex parte`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "from one side"

Done by or for one party without notice to the other. Exceptional, because it departs from the norm of adversarial notice and hearing.

*In use:* The judge granted an ex parte temporary restraining order.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ex_parte) · related: notice, temporary restraining order

### `ex post facto`

**Constitutional / Criminal Law** · lit. "after the fact"

A law that criminalizes conduct retroactively or increases the punishment for an act already done. Both Congress and the states are barred from passing such laws.

*In use:* Raising the penalty after conviction raised an ex post facto problem.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ex_post_facto) · related: retroactivity, bill of attainder

### `habeas corpus`

**Criminal / Civil Procedure** · lit. "that you have the body"

A writ ordering an official who holds a person in custody to justify the detention before a court. The core procedural check on unlawful imprisonment.

*In use:* He petitioned for a writ of habeas corpus after his appeals were exhausted.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/habeas_corpus) · related: writ, collateral review

### `in personam`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "against the person"

Jurisdiction or a judgment aimed at a specific person, imposing personal liability. Contrast `in rem`.

*In use:* With minimum contacts established, the court had in personam jurisdiction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_personam) · related: personal jurisdiction, minimum contacts

### `in rem`

**Civil Procedure / Property** · lit. "against the thing"

Jurisdiction or a judgment aimed at property itself and binding the world as to that property. Contrast `in personam`.

*In use:* The forfeiture proceeded in rem, against the vessel rather than its owner.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/in_rem) · related: in personam, forfeiture

### `inter alia`

**General** · lit. "among other things"

Signals that a list is only partial. Common in pleadings, opinions, and citations.

*In use:* The complaint alleged, inter alia, fraud and breach of contract.

### `mens rea`

**Criminal Law** · lit. "guilty mind"

The mental element of a crime — commonly purpose, knowledge, recklessness, or negligence. Pairs with `actus reus`.

*In use:* The statute's word "knowingly" fixed the required mens rea.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mens_rea) · related: actus reus, scienter

### `nolo contendere`

**Criminal Law** · lit. "I do not wish to contest"

A plea accepting conviction and punishment without admitting guilt. Unlike a guilty plea, it generally cannot be used as an admission in a later civil suit.

*In use:* He pleaded nolo contendere to limit his civil exposure.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/nolo_contendere) · related: plea, guilty plea

### `per curiam`

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "by the court"

An opinion issued in the name of the whole court rather than a single named author. Often short, though not always unanimous.

*In use:* The Court reversed in a brief per curiam opinion.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/per_curiam) · related: opinion, majority opinion

### `per se`

**General** · lit. "by itself"

By itself; inherently. A *per se* rule applies automatically, without case-by-case balancing — for example, negligence per se, or per se antitrust violations.

*In use:* Horizontal price-fixing is a per se violation of the Sherman Act.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/per_se) · related: negligence per se, rule of reason

### `prima facie`

**General** · lit. "at first appearance"

On its face; enough to establish a fact or claim unless rebutted. A *prima facie case* shifts the burden of production to the opponent.

*In use:* The plaintiff made out a prima facie case of discrimination.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/prima_facie) · related: burden of proof, rebuttable presumption

### `pro bono`

**Professional Responsibility** · lit. "for the good" (pro bono publico)

Legal work done without charge for the public good.

*In use:* The firm handled the appeal pro bono.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pro_bono) · related: access to justice

### `pro se`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "for oneself"

Representing oneself in court without a lawyer.

*In use:* The prisoner filed a pro se habeas petition.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pro_se) · related: right to counsel

### `quantum meruit`

**Contracts / Restitution** · lit. "as much as he deserved"

A restitution measure allowing recovery of the reasonable value of services when there is no enforceable contract but one party would otherwise be unjustly enriched.

*In use:* With no signed agreement, the contractor sued in quantum meruit for work performed.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quantum_meruit) · related: unjust enrichment, quasi-contract

### `quid pro quo`

**General** · lit. "this for that"

A reciprocal exchange — the "something for something" at the heart of a bargain (contract consideration), or, in other contexts, bribery and certain harassment claims.

*In use:* The promise lacked a quid pro quo, so there was no consideration.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/quid_pro_quo) · related: consideration, bargain

### `ratio decidendi`

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "the reason for deciding"

The rule or principle the court's decision actually rests on — the binding core of the case, contrasted with `dictum`.

*In use:* The ratio decidendi was narrow, reaching only public employees.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ratio_decidendi) · related: holding, dictum

### `res ipsa loquitur`

**Torts** · lit. "the thing speaks for itself"

Lets a plaintiff prove negligence from circumstance alone when the harm does not normally occur without negligence, the instrument was in the defendant's exclusive control, and the plaintiff did not contribute to it.

*In use:* Counsel invoked res ipsa loquitur after the sponge was left inside the patient.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_ipsa_loquitur) · related: negligence, burden of proof

### `res judicata`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "a matter judged"

Claim preclusion: a final judgment on the merits bars the same parties from relitigating the same claim. Distinct from issue preclusion (`collateral estoppel`).

*In use:* Res judicata barred the second suit on the same contract.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/res_judicata) · related: collateral estoppel, final judgment

### `respondeat superior`

**Torts / Agency** · lit. "let the superior answer"

An employer is vicariously liable for torts an employee commits within the scope of employment.

*In use:* Under respondeat superior, the company answered for its driver's negligence.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/respondeat_superior) · related: vicarious liability, scope of employment

### `scienter`

**Criminal / Securities Law** · lit. "knowingly"

Guilty knowledge — that an act is wrongful — or an intent to deceive. Central to fraud and securities-fraud claims.

*In use:* The securities-fraud claim failed for want of scienter.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/scienter) · related: mens rea, intent

### `stare decisis`

**Reading Opinions** · lit. "to stand by things decided"

The doctrine that courts follow precedent. It runs vertically (lower courts bound by higher) and horizontally (a court's respect for its own past decisions).

*In use:* Stare decisis weighed against overruling the long-settled rule.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/stare_decisis) · related: precedent, holding

### `sua sponte`

**Civil Procedure** · lit. "of its own accord"

An action a court takes on its own initiative, without a party's request.

*In use:* The court raised the jurisdictional defect sua sponte.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/sua_sponte) · related: subject-matter jurisdiction

### `sui generis`

**General** · lit. "of its own kind"

Unique; in a class by itself, not fitting an existing legal category.

*In use:* The court treated the new digital asset as sui generis.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/sui_generis) · related: classification

### `ultra vires`

**Business Associations** · lit. "beyond the powers"

An act beyond the legal authority of a corporation, officer, or agency.

*In use:* Shareholders attacked the guaranty as ultra vires.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/ultra_vires) · related: corporate powers, agency authority

### `voir dire`

**Evidence / Trial** · French "to speak the truth"

Preliminary questioning — of prospective jurors to expose bias, or of a witness (often an expert) to test qualification before testimony.

*In use:* During voir dire, two jurors were struck for cause.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/voir_dire) · related: jury selection, challenge for cause

## Procedure And Litigation

### `affidavit`

**General**

A written statement of facts sworn to under oath before an officer authorized to administer it. Used to support motions and applications.

*In use:* She backed the motion with an affidavit describing the transaction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/affidavit) · related: declaration, perjury

### `answer`

**Civil Procedure**

The defendant's formal written response to a complaint, admitting or denying each allegation and raising defenses and any counterclaims.

*In use:* The answer denied liability and pleaded the statute of limitations.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/answer) · related: complaint, pleading

### `complaint`

**Civil Procedure**

The plaintiff's initial pleading, which starts a lawsuit by stating the claims and the relief sought.

*In use:* The complaint pleaded three counts and demanded a jury.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/complaint) · related: answer, pleading

### `demurrer`

**Civil Procedure**

A challenge asserting that, even if everything pleaded is true, it states no legal claim. Retained in some states (for example, California); the federal analogue is the Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss.

*In use:* The defendant demurred, arguing the complaint stated no cause of action.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/demurrer) · related: motion to dismiss, pleading

### `deposition`

**Civil Procedure**

Out-of-court sworn testimony taken before trial and recorded, used for discovery and sometimes at trial.

*In use:* At trial the witness contradicted her deposition.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/deposition) · related: discovery, interrogatories

### `discovery`

**Civil Procedure**

The pretrial process by which parties obtain evidence from one another — depositions, interrogatories, document requests, and admissions.

*In use:* The decisive emails surfaced during discovery.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/discovery) · related: deposition, interrogatories

### `injunction`

**Remedies / Equity**

A court order requiring a party to do, or to stop doing, a specific act. An equitable remedy, available when money damages are inadequate.

*In use:* The court enjoined the company from using the mark.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/injunction) · related: equity, irreparable harm

### `interrogatories`

**Civil Procedure**

Written questions one party serves on another that must be answered in writing and under oath. Governed federally by Rule 33.

*In use:* The interrogatories asked her to identify every witness.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/interrogatories) · related: discovery, deposition

### `joinder`

**Civil Procedure**

Combining multiple claims or parties into a single lawsuit. It may be permissive or, when interests require it, compulsory.

*In use:* Joinder of the insurer was proper because the claims shared common facts.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/joinder) · related: parties, counterclaim

### `motion`

**Civil Procedure**

A formal request asking the court for a ruling or order. The moving party is the *movant*.

*In use:* Both sides filed pretrial motions.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/motion) · related: summary judgment, brief

### `personal jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's power over the parties. For an out-of-state defendant it generally requires minimum contacts with the forum such that suit does not offend traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice.

*In use:* A single mail-order sale was not enough for personal jurisdiction.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/personal_jurisdiction) · related: minimum contacts, in personam

### `pleading`

**Civil Procedure**

The formal documents — complaint, answer, reply — in which the parties state their claims and defenses. Federal practice requires a "short and plain statement" of the claim.

*In use:* Evidence comes later; the pleadings only frame the dispute.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/pleading) · related: complaint, answer

### `remand`

**Appeals / Civil Procedure**

To send a case back — from an appellate court to a lower court, or from federal back to state court — for further proceedings.

*In use:* The court reversed and remanded for a new trial.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/remand) · related: reverse, removal

### `removal`

**Civil Procedure**

A defendant's transfer of a case from state court to federal court where federal jurisdiction exists (28 U.S.C. § 1441).

*In use:* The defendant removed the case to federal court on diversity grounds.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/removal) · related: remand, diversity jurisdiction

### `standing`

**Civil Procedure / Constitutional Law**

The requirement that a plaintiff have a concrete, personal stake — injury in fact, causation, and redressability — before a court will hear the claim.

*In use:* Without a concrete injury, the plaintiffs lacked standing.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/standing) · related: injury in fact, justiciability

### `subject-matter jurisdiction`

**Civil Procedure**

A court's power over the *type* of dispute (for example, federal-question or diversity). It cannot be waived and may be raised at any time.

*In use:* Lacking subject-matter jurisdiction, the federal court dismissed the case.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/subject_matter_jurisdiction) · related: personal jurisdiction, diversity jurisdiction

### `summary judgment`

**Civil Procedure**

A judgment entered without trial when there is no genuine dispute of material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.

*In use:* Because the key facts were undisputed, the court granted summary judgment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/summary_judgment) · related: motion, material fact

### `venue`

**Civil Procedure**

The proper geographic court (which district) for a trial — distinct from jurisdiction, which is about power.

*In use:* Venue was improper, so the case moved to the district where the events occurred.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/venue) · related: jurisdiction, forum

### `writ`

**General**

A written order issued by a court directing a person to do or stop doing something — for example, writs of certiorari, mandamus, or habeas corpus.

*In use:* The appellate court issued a writ of mandamus directing the judge to rule.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/writ) · related: certiorari, mandamus

## Doctrine And Terms Of Art

### `bailment`

**Property**

The temporary transfer of *possession* — not ownership — of personal property from a bailor to a bailee for a purpose, after which it is returned.

*In use:* Handing the car to the valet created a bailment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/bailment) · related: possession, conversion

### `collateral estoppel`

**Civil Procedure**

Issue preclusion: once an issue is actually litigated and necessarily decided, the same parties cannot relitigate it in a later suit, even on a different claim. Contrast `res judicata`.

*In use:* Collateral estoppel barred relitigating the negligence issue already decided.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/collateral_estoppel) · related: res judicata, issue preclusion

### `consideration`

**Contracts**

The bargained-for exchange that makes a promise enforceable. Each side must give something of legal value — a benefit or a legal detriment.

*In use:* A bare promise to make a gift fails for lack of consideration.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/consideration) · related: bargain, promissory estoppel

### `easement`

**Property**

A nonpossessory right to use another's land for a specific purpose, such as a right of way.

*In use:* The neighbor held an easement to cross the driveway.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/easement) · related: servitude, fee simple

### `fee simple`

**Property**

The largest possible ownership interest in land — full, of potentially infinite duration, and freely transferable. *Fee simple absolute* is the unqualified form.

*In use:* She owned the parcel in fee simple absolute.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fee_simple) · related: estate, life estate

### `laches`

**Remedies / Equity**

An equitable defense barring relief when a plaintiff's unreasonable delay in suing has prejudiced the defendant.

*In use:* Laches barred the claim after a decade of silence.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/laches) · related: equity, statute of limitations

### `negligence`

**Torts**

Failure to use the care a reasonable person would under the circumstances. The plaintiff must show duty, breach, causation (actual and proximate), and damages.

*In use:* Texting behind the wheel was negligence.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/negligence) · related: duty, proximate cause

### `novation`

**Contracts**

Substituting a new contract or a new party for an existing one, with all parties' assent, which extinguishes the original obligation.

*In use:* The novation released the original borrower once the bank accepted the new one.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/novation) · related: assignment, discharge

### `promissory estoppel`

**Contracts**

Enforces a promise despite the absence of consideration where the promisee reasonably and foreseeably relied to their detriment and injustice can be avoided only by enforcement.

*In use:* Promissory estoppel saved the claim after she quit her job in reliance on the offer.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/promissory_estoppel) · related: consideration, reliance

### `proximate cause`

**Torts**

The legal-cause limit on liability: the harm must be a sufficiently foreseeable, non-remote result of the breach. Distinct from actual ("but-for") cause.

*In use:* The bizarre chain of events broke proximate cause.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/proximate_cause) · related: actual cause, foreseeability

### `tort`

**Torts**

A civil wrong, other than breach of contract, for which the law provides a remedy in damages — for example, negligence, battery, or defamation.

*In use:* The same conduct was both a crime and a tort.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/tort) · related: negligence, damages

## Reading Opinions

### `affirm` / `reverse`

**Appeals**

To *affirm* is for a higher court to uphold the decision below; to *reverse* is to overturn it. A reversal is often paired with a `remand` for further proceedings.

*In use:* The court of appeals affirmed on liability but reversed on damages.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/affirm) · related: remand, vacate

### `appellant` / `appellee`

**Appeals**

The *appellant* is the party who appeals a judgment; the *appellee* defends it. Either the original plaintiff or defendant can be the appellant.

*In use:* As appellant, the losing defendant filed the opening brief.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/appellant) · related: petitioner, brief

### `concurrence` (concurring opinion)

**Reading Opinions**

An opinion by a judge who agrees with the result but not (fully) with the majority's reasoning. Persuasive, not binding.

*In use:* The concurrence would have reached the same result on narrower grounds.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/concurring_opinion) · related: majority opinion, dissent

### `dissent` (dissenting opinion)

**Reading Opinions**

An opinion by a judge who disagrees with the majority's result. Not binding, but can shape later law.

*In use:* The dissent warned the rule would prove unworkable.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/dissenting_opinion) · related: majority opinion, concurrence

### `headnote`

**Reading Opinions**

A publisher-written summary of a case's key points, placed above the opinion. A finding aid only — not part of the court's decision and not authority.

*In use:* Cite the opinion itself, never the headnote.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/headnote) · related: opinion, reporter

### `holding`

**Reading Opinions**

The court's actual ruling on the legal issue — the part that binds later courts. Contrast `dictum`.

*In use:* Read narrowly, the holding decided only the standing question.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/holding) · related: dictum, ratio decidendi

### `majority opinion`

**Reading Opinions**

The opinion joined by more than half the judges deciding a case; it states the court's `holding` and is the binding law of the case.

*In use:* The majority opinion drew five votes.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/opinion) · related: concurrence, dissent

### `petitioner` / `respondent`

**Appeals**

In a case brought by petition (for example, on certiorari), the *petitioner* seeks review and the *respondent* opposes it. On appeal, the petitioner is usually the party who lost below.

*In use:* The petitioner sought reversal; the respondent defended the judgment.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/petitioner) · related: appellant, certiorari

### `plaintiff` / `defendant`

**Civil Procedure**

The *plaintiff* brings the suit; the *defendant* is the party sued. In criminal cases the prosecuting party is the government (the *State* or *United States*).

*In use:* The plaintiff bears the burden of proving each element.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/plaintiff) · related: complaint, answer

### `slip opinion`

**Reading Opinions**

The court's first, individually released version of a decision, issued before it appears in a bound reporter. The text is official; pagination is not yet final.

*In use:* Cite the slip opinion until the reporter citation is available.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/opinion) · related: reporter, headnote

### `vacate`

**Appeals**

To set aside or annul a prior judgment or order, wiping out its legal effect — often followed by a `remand`.

*In use:* The court vacated the sentence and remanded for resentencing.

See: [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/vacate) · related: reverse, remand
